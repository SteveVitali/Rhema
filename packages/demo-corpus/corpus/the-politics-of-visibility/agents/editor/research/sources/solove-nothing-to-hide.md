# Solove, "'I've Got Nothing to Hide' and Other Misunderstandings of Privacy" (2007)

- **URLs:** canonical https://digital.sandiego.edu/sdlr/vol44/iss4/5/ (abstract read); full text read via mirror https://vdoc.pub/documents/ive-got-nothing-to-hide-and-other-misunderstandings-of-privacy-28f3qtmr8240
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-21
- **Cite:** Daniel J. Solove, 44 San Diego L. Rev. 745 (2007).

**Establishes:** The standard refutation the draft's SXIII re-runs. Solove steelmans the argument (limited disclosure of innocuous data to officials/computers is no threat to the law-abiding), then answers with privacy's plurality: aggregation ("when combined, the information becomes much more telling"), exclusion, secondary use, distortion; structural rather than visceral harms ("most privacy problems lack dead bodies"); conclusion: "the nothing to hide argument, in the end, has nothing to say." Draft's §XIII diverges by centering *political formation* and association rather than Solove's taxonomy — a genuine inflection — but the section is unciteably close to this famous essay to leave it unnamed.
