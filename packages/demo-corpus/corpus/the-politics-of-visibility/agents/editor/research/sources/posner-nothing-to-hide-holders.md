**Establishes:** §XIII attacks no straw man. Posner, verbatim: "Much of what passes for the name of privacy is really just trying to conceal the disreputable parts of your conduct"; "If the NSA wants to vacuum all the trillions of bits of information that are crawling through the electronic worldwide networks, I think that's fine"; "Privacy interests should really have very little weight when you're talking about national security." Etzioni holds a more sophisticated communitarian version (privacy yields to "well-documented and macroscopic" threats to the common good). Baker's Skating on Stilts argues the "privacy lobby" blocked measures that would have prevented attacks. The sharpest version the draft does NOT answer: Posner's collection/use distinction — machine "vacuuming" is not a harm; harm occurs only at human use, which can be audited.
