# Balkin, "The Constitution in the National Surveillance State" (2008)

- **URL:** https://www.minnesotalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/Balkin_MLR.pdf (retrieved via web.archive.org mirror; landing page https://minnesotalawreview.org/article/essay-constitution-national-surveillance-state/)
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-21 (full text read)
- **Cite:** Jack M. Balkin, 93 Minn. L. Rev. 1 (2008); Lockhart Lecture, Oct. 2006. Companion: Balkin & Levinson, "The Processes of Constitutional Change: From Partisan Entrenchment to the National Surveillance State" (2006).

**Establishes:** The closest scholarly predecessor to the draft's thesis. The National Surveillance State is "a way of governing... neither the product of emergency nor the product of war... a permanent feature of governance," bipartisan, delivered largely by private parties. Three dangers: (1) a "parallel track of preventative law enforcement that routes around the traditional guarantees of the Bill of Rights"; (2) ordinary governance coming to resemble that track; (3) public-private cooperation circumventing constitutional guarantees. Key distinction: "authoritarian information states" (information "gluttons and misers") vs. "democratic information states" (information "gourmets and philanthropists") — a within-democracy gradient that anticipates the draft's capacity/regime decoupling. His prescriptions (collect only what's needed, discard at intervals, "if the information state is unable to forget... it must be able to forgive," oversight structures) anticipate the draft's SXV almost point for point.
