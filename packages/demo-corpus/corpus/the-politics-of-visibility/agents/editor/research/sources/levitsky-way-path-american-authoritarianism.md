# Levitsky & Way, "The Path to American Authoritarianism" (2025)

- **URL:** https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/path-american-authoritarianism-trump ; full text read via https://ash.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Levitsky-Way-2025-The-Path-to-American-Authoritarianism-What-Comes-After-Democratic-Breakdown-3.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-21 (full text read)
- **Cite:** Foreign Affairs, March/April 2025 (pub. Feb. 11, 2025).

**Establishes:** The leading regime-side claim the draft implicitly argues against. US is heading into competitive authoritarianism: "incumbents rig the game by deploying the machinery of government to attack opponents and co-opt critics. Competition is real but unfair." Mechanism is "the politicization and weaponization of government bureaucracy," and the citizen-level effect is *self-sidelining*: "the heightened cost of public opposition will lead many of them to retreat to the political sidelines" — CEOs stop donating, editors soften headlines, universities go quiet. This is the draft's coda mechanism, published 18 months earlier at the elite level. Divergence: L&W say the regime line is being crossed; the draft insists it is not and offers thresholds. Sequel noted (not read): "The Price of American Authoritarianism," Foreign Affairs, Dec. 2025 (https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/american-authoritarianism-levitsky-way-ziblatt). Also of record: Levitsky & Way, "The Dynamics of Autocratic Coercive Capacity After the Cold War" (https://levitsky.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/dynamics-autocratic-coercive-capacity-after-cold-war) — "coercive capacity" is an established term of art.
