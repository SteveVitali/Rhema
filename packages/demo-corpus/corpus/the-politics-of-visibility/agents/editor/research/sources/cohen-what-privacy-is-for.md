# Julie Cohen, "What Privacy Is For" (2013)

- **URL:** https://www.juliecohen.com/attachments/File/CohenWhatPrivacyIsFor.pdf (PDF downloaded and read)
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-21
- **Cite:** 126 Harv. L. Rev. 1904 (2013) (same symposium as Richards).

**Establishes:** The formation argument of §XIII, a decade early. Verbatim: "Privacy is shorthand for breathing room to engage in the processes of boundary management that enable and constitute self-development." "Privacy shelters dynamic, emergent subjectivity from the efforts of commercial and government actors to render individuals and communities fixed, transparent, and predictable." And the thesis-adjacent flourish: "A society that permits the unchecked ascendancy of surveillance infrastructures cannot hope to remain a liberal democracy" — replaced "gradually but surely" by what she calls "modulated democracy." The draft's "privacy that matters politically is a condition of formation" is Cohen's claim with an associational rather than subjectivity-centered grounding.
