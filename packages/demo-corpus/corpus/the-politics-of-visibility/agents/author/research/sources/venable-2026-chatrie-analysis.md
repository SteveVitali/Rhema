# venable-2026-chatrie-analysis

- **URL:** https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/07/supreme-court-holds-geofence-warrant-access · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C5–C13
- **What it establishes:** Law-firm analysis (July 8, 2026): Chatrie v. United States, 6-3, majority by Justice Kagan. Holding: "the government's request for user Location History data through a geofence warrant constituted a 'search' under the Fourth Amendment," relying on Carpenter and reasoning Location History implicates "potentially greater privacy interests than the CSLI in Carpenter." Remanded "the important question of whether the warrant used in Chatrie's case was valid"; left unresolved "what showing law enforcement must make before companies can be compelled to disclose through a geofence warrant."
- **Key passages:** "individuals have a reasonable expectation of privacy in their stored cell-phone location information"; Court rejected arguments that the short period and third-party source negated a search.
- **Reliability:** Secondary; corporate law firm client alert — no advocacy stake in the outcome; good for vote/author and precise holding scope. (Opinion PDF at supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-112_0am4.pdf returned 403 this session; CRS LSB11274 also 403.)
