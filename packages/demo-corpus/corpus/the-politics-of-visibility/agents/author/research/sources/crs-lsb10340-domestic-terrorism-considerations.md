# crs-lsb10340-domestic-terrorism-considerations
- **URL:** https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/LSB/PDF/LSB10340/LSB10340.2.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C14–C19
- **What it establishes:** CRS Legal Sidebar LSB10340, "Domestic Terrorism: Some Considerations" (Aug. 12, 2019, Charles Doyle; version 2). Full PDF downloaded and text-extracted. Establishes that federal law defines domestic terrorism (18 U.S.C. § 2331(5)) without making it a named offense, and that the material-support-to-designated-FTO crime "has no similarly captioned counterpart on the domestic side" — while cautioning that conspiracy/accomplice liability covers much of the same ground.
- **Key passages:** "Existing federal law defines domestic terrorism but does not outlaw it by name." · "Domestic terrorism, by name, is not a federal crime, but the conduct that Section 2331(5) describes is already a state or federal crime under some other name." · "the federal crime of providing material support to a designated foreign terrorist organization has no similarly captioned counterpart on the domestic side. Yet here too, the basis for criminal liability has a different name." · Quoting Holder v. HLP dictum: "[w]e also do not suggest that Congress could extend the same prohibition on material support at issue here to domestic organizations."
- **Reliability:** Primary-adjacent (CRS: nonpartisan congressional shared staff); authoritative on U.S. federal law as of 2019; no advocacy stake.
- **Relevance to this project:** C14 core framing; supports C15/C16 context. Note the sidebar's own caveat (conspiracy/aiding-and-abetting as functional analogues) — the essay should not imply supporters of domestic groups are legally untouchable, only that no designation mechanism/named offense exists.
