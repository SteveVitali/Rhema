# austin-social-legibility-2022
- **URL:** https://ojs.library.queensu.ca/index.php/surveillance-and-society/article/view/15762 · **Accessed:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** v1→v2 response round, differentiating the essay's "behavioral legibility" coinage from prior art
- **What it establishes:** Lisa Austin, "From Privacy to Social Legibility," Surveillance & Society 20:3 (2022), open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). Austin already imports Scott's legibility concept into privacy theory — but at the level of LAW REFORM and DATA GOVERNANCE: she argues privacy law's individual-legibility focus is too narrow and should be enfolded "within a broader data governance framework concerned with the fair and just terms of social legibility." Her project is about governing the terms on which populations are made legible; it is not a descriptive account of legibility produced from behavioral/transactional exhaust. The essay's "behavioral legibility" can be differentiated in one clause as naming the mechanism (legibility assembled from observed behavior) rather than Austin's governance frame — but the essay should cite her as the nearest prior use of Scott-style legibility in privacy theory.
- **Key passages:** Abstract: "This paper draws upon James Scott's Seeing Like a State (1998) to argue that privacy law currently suffers from (at least) three defects: a focus on the legibility of individuals that is too narrow, a focus on collection and subsequent use of data that comes too late, and a focus on rights and harms that ignores the need to create new social structures that can empower more local forms of collective decision-making. What this outlines in broad brushstrokes is the need to enfold privacy concerns within a broader data governance framework concerned with the fair and just terms of social legibility."
- **Reliability:** secondary (peer-reviewed journal article; primary for what Austin herself argues)
- **Relevance to this project:** Settles the originality-audit correction: "social legibility" is Austin's established term; the essay's "behavioral legibility" needs a differentiating clause and a citation to Austin.
