# zuboff-behavioral-surplus

- **URL:** https://findingctrl.nesta.org.uk/text/shoshana-zuboff/ (Shoshana Zuboff, essay/interview for Nesta's "Finding ctrl" collection, 2019, distilling *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** v1→v2 response round, verified definition of "behavioural surplus" and the instrumentarian-vs-totalitarian distinction
- **What it establishes:** Zuboff's own compact definition of behavioural surplus (predictive data beyond what service improvement requires, unilaterally claimed as proprietary) and her explicit claim that surveillance capitalism's "instrumentarian power" is categorically distinct from totalitarian state power — it operates through ubiquitous computational architecture, not terror. Supports both the commercial-surveillance mechanism claims and any contrast the essay draws between market and state modalities of surveillance.
- **Key passages:** (verified verbatim from the Nesta text; note UK spelling "behavioural" in this source — the book uses US "behavioral")
  - Definition: "While some of this data may be applied to product or service improvements, the rest is declared a proprietary behavioural surplus. This surplus is defined by data with substantial predictive value that exceeds what is required for product or service improvement."
  - Scope: "When it comes to 'behavioural surplus' extracted from 'private human experience,' nothing is exempt." And: "The competitive dynamics of these new markets produce economic imperatives to acquire ever more predictive sources of behavioural surplus."
  - Instrumentarian vs. totalitarian: "In this way, surveillance capitalism births a new species of power: instrumentarian power. This is the power to know and shape human behaviour toward others' ends. It is an unprecedented quality of power, completely distinct from totalitarianism. Instead of armaments and armies, terror and murder, instrumentarian power works its will through the automated medium of an increasingly ubiquitous, internet-enabled, computational architecture of 'smart' networked devices, things, and spaces."
  - (Vivid inventory, same source: "Among these growing stores of proprietary surplus, we find your tears, the clench of his jaw in anger, the secrets your children share with their dolls, our breakfast conversations and sleep habits…")
- **Reliability:** Primary for Zuboff's own formulation (authored text under her name, published 2019 by Nesta alongside the book's release); consistent with, but not identical in wording to, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (PublicAffairs, 2019). If the essay cites the book rather than this essay, page-check the book — the book's core definition (ch. 3) is worded differently (e.g., "The rest were considered as a proprietary behavioral surplus…" in the Google-discovery narrative). Cite the Nesta piece for these exact sentences.
- **Relevance to this project:** Bears on sections about commercial surveillance/prediction markets, on distinguishing corporate from state surveillance power, and on any use of the term "behavioral surplus" — which is Zuboff's coinage and must be attributed. Her "completely distinct from totalitarianism" line is a useful counterpoint if the essay argues the two modalities converge.
