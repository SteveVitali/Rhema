# levitsky-way-path-2025
- **URL:** https://ash.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Levitsky-Way-2025-The-Path-to-American-Authoritarianism-What-Comes-After-Democratic-Breakdown-3.pdf (PDF of https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/path-american-authoritarianism-trump) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** v1→v2 response round, the leading regime-side scholarly claim to engage by name
- **What it establishes:** Levitsky & Way, "The Path to American Authoritarianism: What Comes After Democratic Breakdown," Foreign Affairs, March/April 2025 issue, published online Feb. 11, 2025. Full text obtained (PDF, 26 pp.); quotes below verified against the document itself. Establishes: (1) their prediction that U.S. democracy "will likely break down during the second Trump administration"; (2) the competitive-authoritarianism frame — elections continue but the machinery of government is weaponized; (3) the "self-sidelining" mechanism — the heightened cost of public opposition, not jailing, is the engine of authoritarian consolidation. Note: PDF text extraction mangles ligatures (ff/fi/fl/Th render as G/E/H/:); quotes below have ligatures restored, wording otherwise as extracted.
- **Key passages:**
  - "But authoritarianism does not require the destruction of the constitutional order. What lies ahead is not fascist or single-party dictatorship but competitive authoritarianism—a system in which parties compete in elections but the incumbent's abuse of power tilts the playing field against the opposition. ... But the system is not democratic, because incumbents rig the game by deploying the machinery of government to attack opponents and co-opt critics. Competition is real but unfair." (VERIFIED — task's quote matches exactly.)
  - "Although Trump's critics won't be jailed, exiled, or banned from politics, the heightened cost of public opposition will lead many of them to retreat to the political sidelines. In the face of FBI investigations, tax audits, congressional hearings, lawsuits, online harassment, or the prospect of losing business opportunities, many people who would normally oppose the government may conclude that it simply is not worth the risk or effort." (VERIFIED — full wording; note the task's paraphrase omitted "online harassment, or the prospect of losing business opportunities" and the closing "or effort.")
  - "This process of self-sidelining may not attract much public attention, but it can be highly consequential."
  - "the government need not jail its critics to inflict harm on them. Targets of investigation will be forced to devote considerable time, energy, and resources to defending themselves..."
  - On the invisible losses: "it is harder to see the opposition that might have materialized in a less threatening environment but never did—the young lawyers who decide not to run for office; ... the potential whistleblowers who decide not to speak out; the countless citizens who decide not to join a protest or volunteer for a campaign."
  - Closing: "But the opposition can win only if it stays in the game."
- **Reliability:** Primary (the article itself, hosted by Harvard's Ash Center — an authorized institutional copy with Foreign Affairs pagination). Authors are the field's leading scholars of competitive authoritarianism (Levitsky, Harvard; Way, Toronto; co-authors of *Competitive Authoritarianism*, Cambridge UP 2010). Openly critical of the Trump administration — a normative stance, but the analytic framework predates 2024.
- **Relevance to this project:** This is the regime-side scholarly claim to engage by name: the essay's chilling-effects/self-sidelining argument can be anchored to (or distinguished from) Levitsky & Way's mechanism. Their list of instruments (FBI investigations, tax audits, lawsuits) is notably pre-surveillance-tech; the essay's visibility argument extends the mechanism to ambient surveillance.
