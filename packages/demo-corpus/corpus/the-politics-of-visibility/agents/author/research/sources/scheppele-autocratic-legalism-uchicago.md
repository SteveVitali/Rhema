# scheppele-autocratic-legalism-uchicago
- **URL:** https://lawreview.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/11%20Scheppele_SYMP_Online.pdf (also https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/uclrev/vol85/iss2/2/) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C22, C48
- **What it establishes:** Full official PDF of Kim Lane Scheppele, "Autocratic Legalism," *University of Chicago Law Review* 85, no. 2 (2018): 545–583. Read in full (39 pp.). Contains her definition of the term, the hollowing-out argument, and the institutional target lists.
- **Key passages (verbatim; PDF-extraction spacing normalized):**
  - Definition, p. 548: "When electoral mandates plus constitutional and legal change are used in the service of an illiberal agenda, I call this phenomenon autocratic legalism." (Footnote 7 credits Javier Corrales's earlier use of the phrase for Venezuela.)
  - p. 547: "the autocrats who hijack constitutions seek to benefit from the superficial appearance of both democracy and legality within their states. They use their democratic mandates to launch legal reforms that remove the checks on executive power, limit the challenges to their rule, and undermine the crucial accountability institutions of a democratic state."
  - p. 550 (Orbán's Hungary): capture "of crucial institutions, such as the judiciary, the media, the prosecutor's office, the tax authority, and the election commission."
  - p. 581: "they stay in power by weakening the opposition support structures like parties and NGOs, by monopolizing the broadcast media to limit public debate, by harassing critics, and by tinkering with the election rules. ... They do not, as a first resort, call out the tanks or declare a state of emergency; they do not enter office with a phalanx of soldiers. Instead they come to power with a phalanx of lawyers."
  - p. 582: "it is too late to use constitutional appeals to fight autocracy at that point because the constitution has become a hollow shell."
  - p. 582: "the new autocrats come to power not with bullets but with laws. They attack the institutions of liberal constitutionalism with constitutional amendments. They carefully preserve the shell of the prior liberal state—the same institutions, the same ceremonies, an overall appearance of rights protection—but in the meantime they hollow out its moral core. Constitutional institutions survive in the same buildings, but their liberal souls have been killed."
- **Reliability:** Primary (official law review PDF).
- **Relevance to this project:** C22 (definition + hollow-shell + targets). Caution: her canonical target lists name judiciary, media, prosecutors, tax authority, election commission, parties, NGOs, civil society; universities appear only via the CEU episode, not in the definitional lists — rescope any "universities" claim accordingly.
