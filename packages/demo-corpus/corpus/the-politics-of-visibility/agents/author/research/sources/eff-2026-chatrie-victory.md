# eff-2026-chatrie-victory

- **URL:** https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/victory-supreme-court-says-constitution-protects-peoples-location-data · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C5–C13
- **What it establishes:** EFF's same-day analysis (June 29, 2026) of Chatrie v. United States. The Court held people have an expectation of privacy in location data revealing physical-world movements; "even short-term surveillance of these movements is a search subject to the Fourth Amendment." Records generated by phone apps "are a user's 'own'" and protected regardless of sharing with third parties — expanding Carpenter (2018), because "even shorter-term surveillance of location data can constitute a search because it can still reveal 'private matters,' including 'a wealth of detail about a person's familial, political, professional, religious, and sexual associations.'" The Court did NOT decide whether the warrant was "reasonable" or whether good faith applied; remanded to the Fourth Circuit. Gorsuch concurred separately.
