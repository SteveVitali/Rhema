# holder-v-humanitarian-law-project
- **URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/08-1498.ZS.html (syllabus, Cornell LII); citation cross-checked at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holder_v._Humanitarian_Law_Project · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C16
- **What it establishes:** Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project, 561 U.S. 1 (2010), decided June 21, 2010, 6–3, majority by Chief Justice Roberts. Held § 2339B constitutional as applied to plaintiffs' proposed support of the PKK and LTTE — including training members to use international law for peaceful dispute resolution and teaching how to petition the UN — while confirming the statute does not reach independent advocacy or mere membership.
- **Key passages (syllabus):** "The material-support statute, §2339B, is constitutional as applied to the particular forms of support that plaintiffs seek to provide to foreign terrorist organizations." · "Independently advocating for a cause is different from the prohibited act of providing a service 'to a foreign terrorist organization.'" · "a person of ordinary intelligence would understand the term 'service' to cover advocacy performed in coordination with, or at the direction of, a foreign terrorist organization." · "§2339B does not prohibit independent advocacy or membership in the PKK and LTTE." · §2339B(h) "makes clear that 'personnel' does not cover advocacy by those acting entirely independently of a foreign terrorist organization."
- **Reliability:** Cornell LII reproduces the official syllabus/opinion — primary. Wikipedia used only to confirm the U.S. Reports citation (561 U.S. 1), vote (6–3), and author; supremecourt.gov and Justia both 403'd this session. LSB10340 (read in full) independently quotes the opinion's dictum: "[w]e also do not suggest that Congress could extend the same prohibition on material support at issue here to domestic organizations."
- **Relevance to this project:** C16; the coordination/independent-advocacy line is the load-bearing distinction — keep the Court's own formulation ("in coordination with, or at the direction of").
