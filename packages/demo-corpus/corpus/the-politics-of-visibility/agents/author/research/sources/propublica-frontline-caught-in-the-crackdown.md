# propublica-frontline-caught-in-the-crackdown
- **URL:** https://www.propublica.org/article/caught-in-crackdown-ice-cbp-doj-trump-arrests-convictions · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C26
- **What it establishes:** ProPublica/FRONTLINE (A.C. Thompson, Gabrielle Schonder; pub. April 14, 2026) identified 300+ protesters and bystanders arrested during immigration sweeps (LA, Chicago, Minneapolis, Charlotte areas) accused of assaulting/interfering with law enforcement; in more than a third of cases prosecutors quickly dismissed charges, declined to file, or lost at trial. Chicago area: of 109 documented arrests, federal prosecutors dropped charges in at least 75 cases. California: 116 cases reviewed, 32 convictions, 38 pending. Officer statements repeatedly contradicted by video.
- **Key passages:** Cuauhtémoc Ortega (chief federal defender, C.D. Cal.): "We've never had a situation where it seems like you arrest first and then try to justify the reasons for the arrests later." Jared Fishman (Justice Innovation Lab): "If the goal of the Trump administration is to keep people out of the streets, then it doesn't matter if the people are getting convicted. I'm sure it's having a chilling effect."
- **Reliability:** Primary investigative journalism (ProPublica + PBS FRONTLINE); methodology stated (court records, social media, prosecutor/defender data, interviews).
- **Relevance to this project:** C26 — arrest-as-punishment thesis; the chilling-effect quote directly supports "coercive consequences independent of conviction."
