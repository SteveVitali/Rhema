# stoycheff-2018-privacy-panopticon
- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1461444818801317 (page blocks fetchers; metadata + publisher-deposited abstract verified verbatim via https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1461444818801317) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C33
- **What it establishes:** Elizabeth Stoycheff, Juan Liu, Kai Xu, Kunto Wibowo, "Privacy and the Panopticon: Online mass surveillance's deterrence and chilling effects," *New Media & Society* (published online 2018-10-08; print 2019). Two experiments: perceived online government surveillance deters not only illegal activities but also lawful political activities online.
- **Key passages (abstract, verbatim from Crossref deposit):**
  - "Study 1 indicates that surveillance significantly deters individuals' intentions to engage in illegal offenses, an effect that extends to political, but not privacy-protective behaviors."
  - "Study 2 retests the pervasiveness of this effect with a sample of Muslims who reside in the United States. Results indicate that restrictive chilling effects are not specific to any one online population, experimental stimuli, or political context."
- **Reliability:** Secondary (peer-reviewed); the load-bearing scope claim — chilling extends beyond illegal behavior to lawful political activity — is in the authors' own abstract.
- **Relevance to this project:** C33 — chilling-effects mechanism; pairs with stoycheff-2016-under-surveillance.
