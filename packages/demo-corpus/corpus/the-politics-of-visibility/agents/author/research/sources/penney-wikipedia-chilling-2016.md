# penney-wikipedia-chilling-2016
- **URL:** https://btlj.org/data/articles2016/vol31/31_1/0117_0182_Penney_ChillingEffects_WEB.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** v1→v2 response round, strengthening chilling-effects evidence with behavioral (non-survey) data
- **What it establishes:** Jonathon W. Penney, *Chilling Effects: Online Surveillance and Wikipedia Use*, 31 Berkeley Tech. L.J. 117 (2016). Full text read (PDF, all 66 pages). Establishes the strongest behavioral (non-self-report) evidence that publicity about mass surveillance is associated with measurably reduced information-seeking: an interrupted time series (ITS) with segmented regression on monthly aggregate view counts for 48 English-Wikipedia articles selected from the DHS "Analyst's Desktop Binder" keyword list under the topic category "Terrorism," over a 32-month window, January 2012 through August 2014 (n = 32 monthly data points), with the June 2013 Snowden/NSA-PRISM revelations as the "interruption"/exogenous shock. Analysis in Stata with Prais-Winsten auto-correlation correction; no true control group was possible (surveillance potentially affects everyone), but two comparator groups were used (security-related Wikipedia articles; most-viewed Wikipedia articles of 2012–2014), plus overall Wikipedia traffic trends. Core findings, precisely: (1) Non-model comparison of means: average monthly views fell by 526,614 after June 2013 — "approximately a 19.5% drop" — highly statistically significant. [This is the source of the essay's "~20% drop" figure.] (2) First regression results (outliers included): an immediate, statistically significant drop of 995,085 views against a May 2013 total of 2,960,778 — "an immediate drop-off of over 30% of overall views" — but no statistically significant change in the long-term (secular) trend. (3) Second results (two Hamas-article outlier months excluded, per best practice): immediate statistically significant drop of 693,617 views against 2,893,553 — "just under 25%" — AND a statistically significant change in the secular trend of −67,513 monthly views, flipping the trend from +41,421 views/month to −26,092 views/month, i.e., evidence of a sustained, "possibly even permanent," chilling effect. Also establishes the honest caveats: study window ends Aug. 2014; no true control; cannot rule out that users viewed the same content elsewhere; no geographic breakdown of views.
- **Key passages:** "a reduction of 526,614 in the average monthly views for the articles, which represents approximately a 19.5% drop in article view counts" (p. 146). "a reduction of 995,085 views immediately following the June 2013 revelations … an immediate drop-off of over 30% of overall views" (p. 147). "an immediate drop of 693,617 total views. Using the 2,893,553 total article views as of May 2013, this decrease represents an immediate drop-off of just under 25%" (p. 151). "the overall data trend has shifted from an increase of 41,421 views per month to a decrease of 26,092 per month … the NSA/PRISM surveillance revelations are associated with a longer term, possibly even permanent, decrease in web traffic to the Wikipedia pages studied, consistent with a longer term (and possibly permanent) chilling effect" (p. 151). Limitations: "the period of the study only extends until August 2014. This means that the persistence of any chilling effects beyond that point remains an open question" (p. 171); "a true experimental design, one with a true control group … was not possible" (p. 171); "it is impossible to know (because the data is simply not available) whether Wikipedia users chilled or deterred from viewing the articles included in the study were in fact viewing the very same content elsewhere" (p. 171); "article view counts used to construct the time series data set did not distinguish the geographic origins of article views" (p. 172).
- **Reliability:** Primary (the peer-edited law-journal article reporting the original study); author is the researcher (potential motivated interpretation, but limitations are candidly disclosed in Part VI); the underlying data are Wikimedia's public page-view statistics.
- **Relevance to this project:** Anchor citation for upgrading the essay's chilling-effects section from attitudinal/survey evidence to behavioral traffic data. When quoting a headline number, use 19.5% (comparison of monthly means) or ~25%/30% (regression estimates of the immediate level drop, without/with outliers); do not present "20%" as the regression result. The trend-reversal finding (+41,421 → −26,092 views/month) is the strongest available datum for a *sustained* (not merely transient) chill. The limitations paragraph should be used to pre-empt the skeptical counter (see chilling-effects-skeptic-trail note) rather than left for critics to raise.
