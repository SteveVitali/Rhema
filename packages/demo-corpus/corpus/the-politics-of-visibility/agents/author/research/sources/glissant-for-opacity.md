# glissant-for-opacity

- **URL:** https://sites.evergreen.edu/politicalshakespeares/wp-content/uploads/sites/226/2015/12/Glissant-For-Opacity.pdf (scan of "For Opacity," in Édouard Glissant, *Poetics of Relation*, trans. Betsy Wing, Univ. of Michigan Press 1997, pp. 189–194; full chapter verified from the PDF scan) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-25 · **For:** v1→v2 response round, accurate scoping of the "right to opacity" borrowing
- **What it establishes:** Glissant's "right to opacity" is an anti-colonial epistemic and relational claim — against the Western model of "understanding" that comprehends the Other by reducing them to a transparent, comparable object on one's own scale — NOT a claim about state surveillance, data collection, or privacy from government. It grounds Relation among irreducibly singular cultures/persons. Using the phrase for surveillance politics is an extension of his sense and must be flagged as such.
- **Key passages:** (verified against the scanned PDF; obvious OCR ligature errors — e.g. "dernand" for "demand", "irnpossible" for "impossible" — silently corrected below)
  - The famous sentence, which closes the chapter (p. 194): "We clamor for the right to opacity for everyone." Immediately preceding context (p. 194): "This same opacity is also the force that drives every community: the thing that would bring us together forever and make us permanently distinctive. Widespread consent to specific opacities is the most straightforward equivalent of nonbarbarism. We clamor for the right to opacity for everyone."
  - Transparency as reduction (p. 189–90): "If we examine the process of 'understanding' people and ideas from the perspective of Western thought, we discover that its basis is this requirement for transparency. In order to understand and thus accept you, I have to measure your solidity with the ideal scale providing me with grounds to make comparisons and, perhaps, judgments. I have to reduce."
  - Opacity defined against enclosure (p. 190): "Agree not merely to the right to difference but, carrying this further, agree also to the right to opacity that is not enclosure within an impenetrable autarchy but subsistence within an irreducible singularity. Opacities can coexist and converge, weaving fabrics."
  - Opacity as relational, not autistic (p. 190): "The right to opacity would not establish autism; it would be the real foundation of Relation, in freedoms."
  - Chapter opening (p. 189): "Several years back, if I made the statement, 'We demand the right to opacity,' or argued in favor of this, whoever I was speaking to would exclaim indignantly: 'Now it's back to barbarism! How can you communicate with what you don't understand?'"
- **Reliability:** Primary source in the standard English translation (Wing); the PDF is a course-site scan of the University of Michigan Press edition — pagination (189–194) matches the print edition. OCR noise present in the scan; the quotes above are reconstructed from the scan against the well-attested printed text.
- **Relevance to this project:** Bears on any invocation of "opacity" or "the right to opacity" in the essay. Cite as *Poetics of Relation*, trans. Betsy Wing (Ann Arbor: Univ. of Michigan Press, 1997), 189–94; the one-liner is at p. 194. Do not present Glissant as a theorist of state surveillance — mark the transposition explicitly.
