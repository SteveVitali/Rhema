# cbs-doj-drops-law-firm-eo-appeals
- **URL:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/justice-department-drop-defense-trumps-executive-orders-targeting-law-firms/ · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C39
- **What it establishes:** DOJ filed papers in the D.C. Circuit voluntarily dismissing its appeals of the four district-court decisions that struck down Trump's 2025 executive orders against Perkins Coie, Jenner & Block, WilmerHale, and Susman Godfrey; the four judges had ruled the orders violated the First, Fifth, and Sixth Amendments; none of the orders ever took effect.
- **Key passages:** Perkins Coie ruling: the order sent the message that "lawyers must stick to the party line, or else." Judge Loren AliKhan (Susman Godfrey): the order resulted from a "personal vendetta"; the government "sought to use its immense power to dictate the positions that law firms may or may not take."
- **Reliability:** Secondary; CBS News; contemporaneous.
- **Relevance to this project:** C39. CRITICAL caveat from same-session reporting (CBS, NBC, Axios, March 3-6, 2026): days after moving to drop the appeals, DOJ REVERSED COURSE and asked the D.C. Circuit to restore the appeals; consolidated oral argument was held May 14, 2026 (Srinivasan, Pillard, Rao; Paul Clement for the firms). As of 2026-08-20 no D.C. Circuit decision has issued — the district rulings stand but the appeal is pending, NOT abandoned. District judges/dates (via ladd.law.wisc.edu and States United): Beryl Howell struck the Perkins Coie order May 2, 2025 (permanent injunction); John Bates (Jenner & Block, May 23, 2025); Richard Leon (WilmerHale, May 27, 2025); Loren AliKhan (Susman Godfrey, June 27, 2025).
