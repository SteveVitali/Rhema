# stoycheff-2016-under-surveillance
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1077699016630255 (metadata + publisher-deposited abstract verified verbatim via https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1077699016630255) · **Accessed:** 2026-08-20 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C33
- **What it establishes:** Elizabeth Stoycheff, "Under Surveillance" [full running title: Examining Facebook's Spiral of Silence Effects in the Wake of NSA Internet Monitoring], *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly*, published 2016-03-08. The "spiral of silence" surveillance study: perceived surveillance chills willingness to express minority political views online.
- **Key passages (abstract, verbatim from Crossref deposit):**
  - "This study explores how perceptions and justification of surveillance practices may create a chilling effect on democratic discourse by stifling the expression of minority political views."
  - "Using a spiral of silence theoretical framework, knowing one is subject to surveillance and accepting such surveillance as necessary act as moderating agents in the relationship between one's perceived climate of opinion and willingness to voice opinions online."
- **Reliability:** Secondary (peer-reviewed); widely covered 2016 study; sole author Stoycheff.
- **Relevance to this project:** C33 — the 2016 spiral-of-silence result; the 2018 NM&S study (see stoycheff-2018-privacy-panopticon) is the one that shows chilling extends to lawful political behaviors beyond illegal ones.
