# Phase 4 — Structure, prose, voice (editor's working notes)

Draft stage: near-final → full craft depth. Persona files consulted:
`structure-repertoire.md`, `audience-and-venue.md` (not `voice-print.md`, per
convention — findings below are venue-bar findings, not conformity checks).

## Structure

### What works (protect in revision)

- **The opening miniature.** The scare-quotes detail — "The quotation marks
  around *peaceful* are his." — is an elite-grade hook: one piece of punctuation
  carrying the entire thesis in miniature. The pivot at ¶5 ("The word that
  arrives first is *totalitarian*. It is the wrong word, and the ways in which
  it is wrong are the beginning of the analysis") converts the anecdote into the
  method. Stakes are clear well inside the reader's patience budget.
- **§IX's placement** — the strongest objection ("Has America not always done
  this?") is given its own section immediately after §VIII raises it in the
  reader's mind. This is the corpus's own counterargument-gets-a-section norm
  executed well, and the economics answer (scale/persistence/integration/
  automation/retrospectivity/marginal cost) is the essay's best analytic
  passage.
- **§XII's threshold list.** A falsification list, "in both directions," is
  rare in this genre and will be the most-quoted part of the piece. "This
  threshold is categorical: a government that loses lawsuits is a government
  under law…" is exactly the kind of sentence the venue's readers circulate.
- **The §III scoping paragraph** (gait recognition, what Flock does *not* do):
  "an essay that gets the cameras wrong forfeits the standing to be believed
  about the databases" — credibility engineering that distinguishes the piece
  from the genre it will be pattern-matched to.
- **The coda** returns to Minneapolis, completes the epigraph and the title,
  and ends on a stated fact — the corpus's own ending grammar, executed at
  full strength.

### Issues

1. **§XIV interrupts the theoretical ascent XIII → XV.** §XIII ends at the
   constitutional altitude ("almost all of the constitutional future"); §XV
   opens "There remains the hard problem…" — which is the natural continuation
   of §XIII, not of §XIV's practice advice. Consider ordering …XIII → XV
   (design) → XIV (practice) → coda: the theory arc completes, and the
   practice section sits adjacent to the appendix it introduces. Cost: §XIV's
   "public legality is a weapon" currently sets up §XV's institutional theme;
   that sentence would need a new home or the transition rewritten. A genuine
   fork — but as drafted the climb is broken once.
2. **Section count is above the repertoire.** Unnumbered opening + I–XV + coda
   = 17 units against the corpus long-form norm of 10–14
   (`structure-repertoire.md` § Section grammar). Not a defect per se — but it
   is evidence for the merge candidates below rather than against them.
3. **§XI is the declared "philosophical center" and the essay's thinnest
   argumentative section** (~800 words; §X ends by announcing it as the
   center). It runs quote → gloss three times (Tocqueville, Arendt, Shklar)
   and reserves only one paragraph ("Assemble the three and the essay's
   central formulation follows…") for the essay's own move. The two-questions
   formulation ("may I speak?" vs. "what must I reasonably fear if I do?") is
   the piece's best original sentence-pair, and it currently gets four
   sentences of development. See Phase 3 notes — this is argued there as a
   major issue, not just a proportion problem.
4. **§XIII's five-thinkers recap paragraph** ("The essay's five thinkers
   converge on this point from five directions: Scott, because… Foucault,
   because…") is the one place the essay summarizes itself. The venue norm is
   no concession to skimming and the corpus almost never recapitulates (1 of
   10 pieces). The convergence claim is legitimate; the roll-call form is a
   wrap-up list. Candidate: compress to one sentence or cut the enumeration.
5. **Final sentence overstates the essay's own bounded claim.** "Legibility is
   a condition of manipulation. Its absence is a condition of freedom…" — but
   §XIII took care to argue for *practical opacity*, bounded ("no immunity
   from lawful, particularized investigation"), not the *absence* of
   legibility. The last sentence is the pull-quote; as written it hands a
   hostile reader the "so you want the state blind?" reading §XIII explicitly
   foreclosed. Fix candidates: "Its limits are a condition of freedom" /
   "Freedom is a condition of its limits." Small edit, large exposure.

## Prose (patterns with exemplars)

1. **Aphorism-coda density.** The one-sentence paragraph that pays off a
   section is the persona's signature; this draft runs ~11 of them ("The
   machinery can precede the regime." / "The person carries the file." /
   "Inherited capacity does not leave. It waits." / "Anticipation trips none
   of them." / "It has never before been this cheap." …). At this frequency
   the drumbeat becomes audible and each aphorism discounts the next; the
   fidelity audit's own figure (~13% single-sentence paragraphs, above the
   model piece) points the same way. Keep the four or five that carry theses
   ("The machinery can precede the regime"; "The ideology is not the charge.
   It is the query."); fold the others into their paragraphs. Example rewrite:
   end §IV's spyware passage "…the address book, the location history, and the
   key ring at once — the person carries the file." One paragraph, same
   payoff, one less drumbeat.
2. **The "not X. It is Y." template.** The negation-correction is the corpus's
   characteristic sentence, but the draft runs the exact two-sentence template
   at least six times: "The danger is not the eye. It is the interoperability."
   (§III); "The mechanism is not observation. It is uncertainty about
   observation…" (§X); "The ideology is not the charge. It is the query." (§V);
   "The right question about this document is not the indignant one." (§V);
   "Law is not absent from this story; it is behind, which is different."
   (§IV); "The instructive precedents… are not foreign. They are ours." (§VIII).
   Two risks: internal monotony, and — a live reception risk — elite readers
   now pattern-match "It's not X, it's Y" cadence as AI boilerplate and
   discount it. Vary the syntax on all but the two best instances (e.g., "Law
   is not absent from this story; it is behind" already varies it — that's the
   model).
3. **Visible stage machinery.** "That is a claim about architecture, and it is
   why the argument must now turn to the technology." (§I); "The next two
   sections concern the machinery…" (§II); "What, exactly, is lost when the
   meeting shrinks? The next section is the answer, and it is the philosophical
   center of the essay." (§X); "…in ways the next section must be equally
   precise about" (§V); plus ~12 occurrences of "this essay"/"the essay" as
   subject. The corpus keeps through-lines audible with one-sentence
   paragraphs, not with self-narration, and the venue's stated norm is no
   "in this piece I will." Worst instance is §X's close: the question "What,
   exactly, is lost when the meeting shrinks?" is a perfect transition on its
   own — cut the sentence that follows it and the announcement of "the
   philosophical center" (which also raises expectations §XI then undercuts;
   see Structure #3).
4. **Local repetition.** "The phone completes the picture." then "Face
   completes identity's side of the ledger." — same verb, adjacent paragraphs
   (§III). Vary one.

Otherwise the line level is strong: register consistent (no lurches), hedge
stacks absent, jargon earned, quotations integrated in the corpus's
author-and-claim-in-prose style. "Advice detached from a threat model is
costume" (§XIV) and "the delays are where liberty actually lives" (§XV) are
the venue's kind of sentence.

## Voice

There is one, and it is consistent: cold, precise, scoped, counting things
("at a rate of two thousand seven hundred arrests"). It is the right
instrument for an argument whose entire strategy is to out-precision both the
hysterics and the complacent. Authority is earned by scoping discipline — the
Paragon paragraph (§IV) and the Minnesota not-yet-ruled caveats are models;
nowhere does the persona claim expertise the text hasn't established. The one
place the voice risks sliding into self-parody is the aphorism ration (Prose
#1). The piece sounds like *someone* throughout — the danger is only that it
sounds like him slightly too often per thousand words.
