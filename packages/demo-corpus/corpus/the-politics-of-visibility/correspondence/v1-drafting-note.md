## The piece as drafted

- **Title:** *The Politics of Visibility* — *Surveillance and the Conditions
  of Democratic Freedom* (the manifest's working title; the outline's
  internal favorite, *The Architecture of Fear*, was declined as off the
  corpus's title register).
- **Thesis:** authoritarianism is a capacity as well as a regime type; a
  democracy can accumulate the machinery of political control — legally,
  largely through private infrastructure — long before its regime type
  changes; the constitutional question this forces is what the state must be
  structurally prevented from knowing.
- **Length:** ~9,700 words body + coda, ~280-word appendix, 51 numbered
  references (target 10,000–10,500 incl. appendix; corpus ceiling 10,400).
- **Shape:** epigraph (Scott: "Legibility is a condition of manipulation."),
  unnumbered opening ("The Conspiracy"), fifteen roman-numeral sections,
  unnumbered coda ("Freedom Before the Knock") that completes the epigraph
  and title, compact security-baseline appendix.

## Decisions among the outline's alternatives

- Title and subtitle as above; appendix included (austere, links outward)
  rather than spun off as a companion piece.
- Epigraph: Scott rather than Fraenkel (whose definitional sentence is too
  long for the venue's epigraph practice) or Shklar (quoted at full strength
  in §XI instead).
- Section titles adopt the outline's literary alternates where they fit the
  declarative-title grammar ("The Distance Between Danger and Dictatorship").

## Verification: what changed under checking

All 48 inventoried claims were re-verified against fetched sources (six
research subagents; ~90 source notes in `agents/author/research/`). Every
outline citation carried a deep-research tracking parameter and started at zero trust.
Nothing was contradicted outright, but verification materially corrected:

- **The Conspiracy diagram:** CWA and Sunrise Movement are financial-records
  targets but not confirmed diagram entries; the draft keeps them off the
  slide. "Recorded conversations" is Minnesota Reformer's report, attributed.
- **Flock OS Investigate:** WIRED's story rests on code exposed through
  Flock's own login pages (not "obtained code"); stated as such.
- **UK litigation:** the outline's "court upheld the ban" concealed a seesaw
  — the High Court found the proscription unlawful ("ordinary criminal law
  was available"), the Court of Appeal reversed, the Supreme Court will
  decide. The full posture is in the draft and strengthens its
  checks-are-contested theme.
- **The "unconstitutional chilling" finding** belongs to Judge Young in AAUP
  v. Rubio, not to Khalil's own case; attributed accordingly.
- **ICE/Paragon:** the acknowledgment letter names no vendor; DHS later
  denied a relationship; a procurement notice recorded the contract closed
  out — all three complications are carried, and the capability/use
  distinction is explicit.
- **Tocqueville:** the outline's quotation did not match any opened source;
  the draft quotes the verified Reeve rendering ("the mother of science").
- **Church Committee:** the famous five-part "expose, disrupt…" string is
  Book III (unverified this round) and is not used; Book II wording is.
- **ICE funding:** corrected to $75B/4yr, $45B detention, vs. a ~$9–10B prior
  annual budget (Brennan Center).
- **Churches:** the supporting case is Philadelphia Yearly Meeting v. DHS
  (4th Cir., Aug. 18, 2026), not Mennonite (where the PI was denied).
