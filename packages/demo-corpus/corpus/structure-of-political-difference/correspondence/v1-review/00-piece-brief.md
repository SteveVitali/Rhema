## Thesis

The left–right spectrum is a lossy compression of at least five analytically separable political questions (economy, coercion, culture, belonging, system); political language should therefore be rebuilt as a three-layer vocabulary — five-axis coordinates for analysis, prototype family names for speech, and modifiers/tags for what both miss — with left/right retained above it as coalition names rather than explanatory categories.

Stated most strongly: *ideological labels conflate two functions — describing a position and naming a neighborhood — and disagreement becomes tractable only when a public vocabulary separates them.*

## Genre and project

A constructive conceptual essay — part critique of an inherited vocabulary, part proposal of a replacement framework, part usage manual. Closer to a manifesto-with-apparatus than a pure essay: it proposes notation (five-number addresses), a lexicon (14 family names), modifiers, metadata, and rules of use. Success on its own terms = a reader (a) stops treating left/right as explanatory, (b) can actually *use* the five questions and family names in ordinary political talk, and (c) trusts the framework because it is honest about its own limits (Sections II, X).

## Intended audience and venue bar

Educated generalists who read idea-driven Substacks (Sebastian's readership: philosophy/history-of-ideas inflected, comfortable with Freeden and Bourdieu name-checks but not political-science insiders). The venue bar: essays that advance one arresting thesis through continuous, aphoristic, historically textured prose. Notable tension: v1 is far more *apparatus-heavy* (tables, scales, bulleted lexicon, rules) than anything the venue has published.

## Argument skeleton

1. **Motivating failure cases** (intro): Trump/Milei, Sanders/Trump, Obama/Macron show left–right compresses distinct disagreements. → spectrum is "lossy compression."
2. **§I Morphology:** Ideologies are arrangements of contested concepts (Freeden), not essences; party families are real but fuzzy (Mair/Mudde). → separate coordinates (position) from names (neighborhood). Rejects "32-vertex" exhaustive naming.
3. **§II Anti-naturalism disclaimer:** the five questions are analytically separable, not empirically independent; counterfactual-independence test; CHES 2024 exploratory composites show culture/coercion/belonging bundle (~.78–.82), economy semi-independent (~.39–.56), anti-establishment cross-cutting.
4. **§III The five axes** defined with anti-conflation caveats (economy ≠ government-vs-freedom; coercion ≠ state size; culture ≠ coercion; belonging ≠ ethnic nationalism; system ≠ populism simpliciter).
5. **§IV Populism decomposed** (Mudde thin-centered; four facets: anti-establishment, directism, pluralism, legitimation; Caramani populism/technocracy) → hierarchical solution: public gloss (Populist↔Establishment) over rigorous facets.
6. **§V Notation:** 5-number address + metadata (salience, confidence, time, object) + rhetoric/program/practice layers; no master score.
7. **§VI–VII Lexicon and grammar:** 14 prototype families + 9 modifiers, one-modifier rule; three-layer descriptions (Trump, Obama, Milei, Orbán examples).
8. **§VIII What left/right still do:** heuristic/coordination function (cite [^1]); bundles are historically contingent; left/right = coalition names *above* the coordinates.
9. **§IX Names are historical, coordinates portable** (Pew 2026 typology as illustration).
10. **§X Exclusions:** regime type, ecology, foreign policy, centralization, religion, capacity.
11. **§XI Rules of use** (evidence hierarchy, salience, uncertainty, dating, no cross-axis inference, classify arguments, charitable poles).
12. **§XII–XIII 'Better sentences' payoff and closing wager.**

Load-bearing moves: (a) the compression metaphor; (b) Freeden as licence for prototype-not-checklist naming; (c) the CHES exercise as empirical footing; (d) the populism decomposition as proof the method adds resolution; (e) the claim that this five-fold partition in particular is the right decompression (mostly implicit — the piece never argues *why five and these five*).

## Research agenda (for Phase 2)

The piece never cites the large existing family of multi-axis political taxonomies — its nearest neighbors. Map at minimum:
- **Popular/online:** Nolan chart & Political Compass (2 axes); 8values (4 axes: economic, diplomatic, civil, societal — strikingly close to questions 1/4/2/3); 9Axes; PolitiScales; Arnold Kling's *Three Languages of Politics*.
- **Academic:** Eysenck (R/T dimensions); Rokeach two-value model; Hooghe/Marks GAL–TAN; Kitschelt's left-libertarian vs right-authoritarian diagonal; Inglehart–Norris cultural backlash; Bobbio *Left and Right*; Converse on constraint; multidimensional scaling literature already partly cited [^4].
- **Direct predecessors on 'left-right is a fiction/compression':** Hyrum & Verlan Lewis, *The Myth of Left and Right* (2023) — likely the strongest published neighbor/counterargument (their claim: there are no essential dimensions at all, only tribal bundles — which cuts *against* five real questions); Scott Alexander / SSC-adjacent discourse on political taxonomies; Freeden himself on the limits of spatial metaphors.
- **Populism decomposition:** Mudde/Kaltwasser; Müller *What Is Populism?* (exclusive-representation claim — piece's "moral dualism/unitarism" facets overlap); Norris/Inglehart's authoritarian-populism measures; V-Dem/POPPA expert measures.
- **Strongest existing counterarguments to find:** (a) Lewis-style anti-essentialism (if bundles are tribal, five "questions" may be just another reification); (b) Converse/mass-publics — ordinary citizens lack structured ideology, so a five-axis public vocabulary misdescribes what vocabularies do; (c) dimensional-reduction defenses (Poole–Rosenthal: one or two dimensions statistically suffice for elite behavior).
- **Reception:** political-science Twitter/Substack (e.g., Broockman/Fowler-adjacent empiricists), rationalist-adjacent readers who know 8values, and left/right partisans who will contest the worked profiles (esp. Trump "market-leaning," Modi as natcon).

## Notes for Phase 3 (flags formed on first read — to be tested, not assumed)

- The piece never argues **why five** — why coercion and culture are two questions but ecology is a "module"; the anti-naturalism disclaimer (§II) does a lot of insulating work and may verge on unfalsifiability ("distinct enough to separate; history binds them" = every correlation pattern confirms).
- Potential tension: §II says composites correlate .78–.82 (culture/coercion/belonging) — a critic will say that *is* one dimension in Europe, so the framework's added resolution is theoretical, not empirical, exactly where the piece claims empirical footing.
- The lexicon (14 families) is asserted, not derived; criteria for inclusion ("recurring and politically consequential neighborhoods") are not applied visibly.
- Genre risk at this venue: manual vs essay.
