# Craft Notes (Phase 4) — "Politics Has Five Questions" v1

Venue calibration source: `agents/editor/research/sources/sjsebastian-substack-style.md`. The venue publishes continuous, aphoristic, historically textured argumentative essays in roman-numeral sections, impersonal authoritative voice, near-zero formal apparatus. The writer has overridden the venue's no-citations habit ([n] endnotes required), which is fine — but the other deltas below are real.

## Structure

**Opening.** Strong and venue-apt. The compression conceit ("The left–right spectrum is not false. It is a compression.") is exactly the kind of arresting inversion the venue trades in, and the three example pairs (Trump/Milei, Sanders/Trump, Obama/Macron) earn the next five minutes. "The aim is not political neutrality. It is political resolution." is a keeper. No bounce risk before §I.

**The manual problem (the big one).** The piece changes genre mid-stream. §§I–IV are an essay: history of ideas, conceptual argument, the populism decomposition. §§V–VII and X–XI are a *user manual*: a scoring scale table, metadata checklists, a 14-entry glossary, a modifier list, six exclusion subsections, seven numbered rules of use. Nothing at this venue looks like the second half. A Substack reader who came for the essay will skim from §V onward; the lexicon's fourteen consecutive dictionary entries (§VI) are the single largest pacing dead zone. The package already contains `taxonomy_reference_sheet.md` — the apparatus has a home that is not the essay's body. Direction: keep the *argument* for each apparatus choice (why no master score; why prototype families; why one modifier), compress the *inventory* (discuss the 4–6 families that do argumentative work — socdem/demsoc as a minimal pair, welfare nationalist and natcon as the proof that left/right fails, libertarian via Milei — and gesture at the rest via the figure and reference sheet).

**Sequencing.** §II ("Not Five Natural Dimensions") disclaims the five questions before the reader has seen them; the header refers to something not yet on stage. Either fold the disclaimer into the presentation (§III) or move the CHES material after the questions so the reader knows what was correlated. Currently the reader meets ".78 to .82" correlations among "culture, coercion, and belonging" two pages before learning what those words mean here.

**Repetition.** The "left and right are coalition names / the five questions are contents" point is made in the intro, §VIII (twice, once as a pull quote), §IX, and §XIII. The file-name analogy (§VIII) and the compression metaphor (intro) are the same idea in two costumes; §XIII recapitulates §VIII's list of anomalous combinations almost verbatim ("market radicals allied with cultural traditionalists…" ≈ intro's and §VIII's examples). One full statement plus one closing echo is enough.

**Sections earning their place.**
- §I, §IV, §VIII, §IX: the essay's spine; strongest material; protect.
- §II: needed but mislocated (see above).
- §V: the no-master-score argument is essential; the 1–5 scale table and metadata bullets are reference-sheet material.
- §VI: compress hard (see manual problem).
- §VII: the three-layer grammar and the four worked one-sentence profiles are the payoff of the whole piece — this is what readers will quote. Keep all four, but they arrive after 3,000 words of apparatus; consider letting one appear early as a promissory note.
- §X: honest and trust-building, but six subsections of exclusions is inventory; two paragraphs could do the work of the section's argument (a taxonomy becomes untrustworthy when it hides its exclusions; regime type is the non-negotiable separate tag).
- §XI: rules 5 and 7 (no cross-axis inference; charitable pole definitions with the good/fear table) are genuinely argumentative; rules 1–4 and 6 are manual.
- §XII: excellent, venue-perfect, the "instead/say" pairs are the demonstration that the framework changes sentences. Keep whole.
- §XIII: good final cadence; trim the recapitulation.

**Ending.** The last five lines land ("Coordinates before categories. Questions before camps." / "Not political neutrality. Political precision.") — symmetric with the intro, venue-apt.

## Prose (patterns with exemplars)

**P1 — "not X … (it is) Y" saturation.** The venue's signature inversion, but v1 runs it at a density that reads as tic — and, in 2026, as AI-cadence, which elite readers discount. Count: ≥15 instances. Exemplars: "The aim is not political neutrality. It is political resolution." (intro — keep); "An ideology, on this view, is not one answer. It is an arrangement of answers." (§I — keep); "It is not a disguised measure of government size." (§III.2); "The distinction is not moral seriousness against permissiveness." (§III.3); "Nor is universalism simply kindness." (§III.4); "These distinctions are not bureaucratic ornament." (§V); "A prototype is not a checklist. It is a recognizable center of gravity." (§VI); "This is not semantic fastidiousness." (§XII). Direction: ration to the ~5 strongest (intro, §I, §VI, closing); rewrite the rest as positive assertions — e.g., "The axis records the governing ideal, not the actual outcome"-type constructions already present do the work without the drumbeat.

**P2 — Apparatus intrusions into prose.** Tables (§V scale, §XI pole table), block-quote Q&A devices (§VII: three consecutive "should answer:" quote blocks), and bolded axiom pull-quotes ("**Left and right are coalition names…**"). The pole table (§XI.7) is defensible — it is an argument about symmetry. The 1–5 scale table is not essay material. The §VII Q&A blocks can collapse into one paragraph of running prose.

**P3 — Compression to the point of assertion.** Some aphorisms outrun their support: "A vocabulary succeeds only when people can think with it." (§I) and "Labels gain explanatory power by excluding detail." (§VII) are good; but e.g. "Coordinates are exact enough for analysis. Names are short enough for speech." (§I) does triple duty as thesis, slogan, and section close while the essay never shows a coordinate *failing* in speech or a name failing in analysis until §VII — the demonstration lags the slogan by five sections.

**P4 — Line-level errata.**
- §VI Socdem entry: "less committed to replacing capitalism than the **democ**" — typo for "demsoc."
- §V: "a 2–3–1–2–4" vs §V's earlier "2 · 4 · 1 · 2 · 5" and reference sheet's "4 · 5 · 4 · 5 · 1" — notation format wobbles (en-dashes vs interpuncts).
- §III.5 pull-quote "Populist ↔ Establishment" is introduced as "the familiar gloss" before §IV argues for it — forward reference is fine but currently reads as conclusion-before-argument.
- Figure references are bare filenames with no captions/alt text; Substack will need uploaded images and captions (the CHES figure especially needs its "exploratory, not official CHES" caveat *in the caption*, not only in body text).
- "directism" is a coinage (rare in the literature); it works, but it should be flagged as a term of art on first use — v1 half-does this.

**P5 — First person.** Used exactly once ("I constructed exploratory composites," §II) — strategic, matches venue precedent ("After Bureaucracy"). Keep singular; do not add more.

## Voice

There is one, and it is the right one: impersonal, diagnostic, legislative — the voice of someone founding a vocabulary rather than opining. It is consistent with the venue's "diagnosis rather than prescription" authority. Two places it wavers:
- §VI entries drop into reference-entry register ("The term is short for democratic socialist…") — encyclopedia, not essay.
- §XI numbered-rule register ("Record which.") — style-guide imperative; fine in a reference sheet, cold in the essay.
The authority is largely earned — the piece is honest about limits (§II, §X) in a way that buys its confidence elsewhere. Unearned spots: the worked profiles (§VII) assert contestable scorings (Trump "market-leaning") with the same legislative confidence as the definitional material; a reader who rejects one scoring will discount the framework. The profiles need one sentence acknowledging they are illustrations of the *grammar*, dated and disputable, not verdicts.

## Substack-mechanical notes

- Length ~6,100 words: long but within venue norms; the manual-compression above will bring it toward the venue's ~4,500–5,500 sweet spot.
- Footnote syntax: draft uses `[^n]` markdown footnotes; the writer requires `[n]` bracket citations with a numbered reference list at the end (Substack renders `[^n]` poorly when pasted; bracket style is robust). Renumber in order of first appearance; several references are cited from multiple sections (CHES cited as [^5] twice) — bracket style handles this fine.
- Pull-quote blocks (`>`) render as blockquotes on Substack — currently used for emphasis ~10 times; halve.
