# caplan-lewis-exchange

- **URL:** https://www.betonit.ai/p/the-myth-of-left-and-right-the-last · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v1 review Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Bryan Caplan defends the spectrum as a useful simple model: "the spectrum helps us to understand political identity and political conflict. Which it totally does" (analogy: masculine/feminine, rich/poor — imperfect unidimensional models can still be useful). Hyrum Lewis's last-word rebuttal: "the political spectrum is not useful...it's based on a delusion"; studying how people self-identify around false categories differs from the categories modeling reality. Crucially for Phase 2, Lewis states the granularity objection: "there is more than one issue in politics" — politics is like biology (many species) or chemistry (many elements); we should keep issues unbundled — "why not just say 'anti-market' instead of 'left-wing'?"
- **Key passages:** As quoted above; both sides in one exchange.
- **Reliability:** Primary for both positions (hosted debate on Caplan's Substack).
- **Relevance to this project:** Contains, in compact form, both the strongest practical defense of left-right (Caplan's coordination/simple-model view — which the draft's §VIII partly concedes) and the strongest attack on the draft (Lewis's granularity argument, which applies with equal force to five axes: each of the draft's "questions" is itself a bundle — Economy bundles trade, tax, redistribution, property; a protectionist tax-cutter has no single Economy score). The draft addresses neither party by name.
