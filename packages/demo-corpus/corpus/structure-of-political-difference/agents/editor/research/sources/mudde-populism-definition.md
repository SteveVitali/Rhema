# mudde-populism-definition

- **URL:** https://amc.sas.upenn.edu/cas-mudde-populism-twenty-first-century (the draft's own [^8]) · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v1 review Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Mudde defines populism as "a thin-centered ideology that considers society to be ultimately separated into two homogenous and antagonistic groups: 'the pure people' and 'the corrupt elite'" and which "argues that politics should be an expression of the volonté générale (general will) of the people." Core features: monism and moralism; thin-centeredness requires host ideologies (nationalism on the right, socialism on the left); populism is "an illiberal democratic response to democratic illiberalism," challenging minority rights, pluralism, separation of powers.
- **Key passages:** As quoted.
- **Reliability:** Primary (Mudde's own text at the cited URL).
- **Relevance to this project:** Misrepresentation check passes: the draft's §IV summary (thin-centered; pure people vs corrupt elite; general will) is accurate, and its three-claim decomposition (anti-establishment, moral dualism, directism) is a fair unpacking. Note: "homogenous groups" supports the draft's fourth claim (unitarism), but the exclusive-moral-representation version of unitarism is Müller's signature claim, not Mudde's — the draft should credit Müller (see muller-what-is-populism note).
