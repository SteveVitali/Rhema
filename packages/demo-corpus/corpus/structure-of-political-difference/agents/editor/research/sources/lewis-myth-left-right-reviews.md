# lewis-myth-left-right-reviews

- **URL:** https://medium.com/@cbaylor/reviewing-the-myth-of-left-and-right-by-hyrum-and-verlan-lewis-5644a509999d and https://kirkcenter.org/reviews/essentially-lying-about-the-left-and-right/ · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v1 review Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Hyrum Lewis & Verlan Lewis, *The Myth of Left and Right* (OUP 2023), argue against "ideological essentialism": there is no enduring philosophy or essence uniting the positions bundled as left or right. Their "social theory": "people choose their political affiliation for social reasons and then reverse-engineer their positions on specific issues to fit within that group" (Baylor); "people adopt the positions of a tribe first and only then invent a story that ties all those positions together" (Trepanier). "Ideologies... are reverse engineered to fit tribal actions and attachments."
- **Key passages:** Baylor's objections: sticky ideologues who keep ideology against party drift; independent thinkers mitigate tribalism; psychological substrates (personality/neuro research) may partly explain ideological difference across countries. Neither review engages the question of replacing one dimension with several real ones.
- **Reliability:** Two independent secondary sources (one academic-adjacent political scientist, one conservative review organ); consistent with each other and with the authors' own essays.
- **Relevance to this project:** The nearest popular predecessor of the draft's "left/right are coalition names, not explanations" — and simultaneously the strongest counterargument to positing five *real* questions: if bundles are tribal artifacts, a five-axis scheme is a bigger reification, and the Lewises explicitly resist small-n dimensional replacements (see caplan-lewis-exchange note). The draft cites neither the book nor the argument.
