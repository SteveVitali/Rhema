# muller-what-is-populism

- **URL:** https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/lsereviewofbooks/2016/12/14/book-review-what-is-populism-by-jan-werner-muller/ (review read); publisher/interview corroboration via https://booksandideas.net/Populism-is-a-Form-of-Anti-Pluralism and https://jwmueller.princeton.edu/publications/what-populism (search results) · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v1 review Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Müller's *What Is Populism?* (2016): anti-elitism is necessary but not sufficient; the defining criterion is **anti-pluralism** — populists "claim that they, and they alone, represent the people," a moral monopoly of representation excluding others from "the people." Populism is "a specific form of identity politics that is critical of elites, anti-pluralist and has a moral claim of representation"; the claims are "moral and symbolic rather than empirically testable." Reviewer criticisms: weak operationalization, reliance on hypotheticals.
- **Key passages:** As quoted.
- **Reliability:** Secondary (LSE review) plus consistent search-level corroboration; book itself not read this session.
- **Relevance to this project:** The draft's facets "moral dualism," "unitarism," and "pluralism: are opposition and competing interests permanently legitimate?" are Müller's exclusive-representation/anti-pluralism apparatus in all but name, and Müller appears nowhere in the draft. This is the clearest attribution gap in the populism section — easily fixed with one citation, and dangerous unfixed because *What Is Populism?* is the single best-known trade book on the subject among the draft's likely readers.
