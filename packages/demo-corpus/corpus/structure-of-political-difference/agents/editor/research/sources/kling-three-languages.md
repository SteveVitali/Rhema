# kling-three-languages

- **URL:** https://quillette.com/2018/08/08/the-three-languages-of-politics-a-review/ (review, read); overview results via https://www.econtalk.org/kling-on-the-three-languages-of-politics/ · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v1 review Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Arnold Kling's *Three Languages of Politics* (2013; 3rd ed. 2019): progressives, conservatives, and libertarians speak along three moral axes — oppressor↔oppressed, civilization↔barbarism, liberty↔coercion. Crucially these are *languages/heuristics for framing issues*, not positions: "each tribe is simply focused on a different set of concerns" which "can also be compatible."
- **Key passages:** Kling: "treating people who use other heuristics as reasonable is likely to prove a less stressful" approach. The three tribes are "like tribes speaking different languages… most political commentary serves to increase polarization."
- **Reliability:** Secondary (favorable review); consistent with Kling's own summaries across sources.
- **Relevance to this project:** Nearest popular predecessor for the draft's *grammar* framing ("We do not need a more complicated compass. We need a grammar"). Kling's axes map loosely onto the draft's poles (oppressor↔oppressed ≈ Progress/Equality-pole moral logic; civilization↔barbarism ≈ Tradition/Authority-pole; liberty↔coercion ≈ Liberty pole). Key difference the draft can exploit: Kling describes rhetorical heuristics of three American tribes; the draft describes positional coordinates plus naming conventions. But the draft's "charitable pole definitions" table (each pole = a good pursued + a failure feared) is very close to Kling's method of steelmanning each language, and should cite him.
