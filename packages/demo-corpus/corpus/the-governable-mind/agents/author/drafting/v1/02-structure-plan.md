# Structure plan — The Governable Mind, v1

- **Fidelity:** adaptive. **Target:** ~11,500 body words (tolerance 12,000).
- **Reading order vs. outline order:** the outline's seventeen units are kept
  in number and, with one exception (D3 below), in sequence. The outline's
  order already is a reading order: puzzle (prologue) → what liberalism
  protected (I) → why the protected thing is leaving the skull (II) → what
  it becomes outside (III) → which freedoms are at stake (IV) → whose power
  (V) → how control has historically been built (VI) → what the new
  legibility is (VII) → what it can do when unconstrained (VIII) → how it
  shapes rather than forbids thought (IX) → the objection at full force (X)
  → the technical decomposition that dissolves the false binary (XI) → the

  (XIV) → the alternative (XV) → coda. The re-budgeting, not the re-ordering,
  is where this plan departs from the outline.

## The spine (twelve steps, each a claim)

1. The same incident showed why people want AI governed and why it is
   dangerous for intelligence to be governable only through institutions; so
   the prior question is what is being controlled when AI participates in
   thought.
2. Liberalism's protection of the inner life was built, contested, and
   partial: born from the discovery that sincere certainty does not license
   coercion (Bayle), fixed as a limit at judgment (Spinoza), bounded (Locke),
   aimed at licensing rather than speech (Milton), reframed as non-delegated
   reason (Kant), and given a theory of truth under fallibility (Mill).
3. Cognition was never confined to the skull, and the political argument
   needs only the weak claim (functional integration), not the strong one
   (constitution); what leaves the skull can be recorded.
4. Records generated during thought are not thoughts, but the political
   protection of thought never depended on identity between a medium and the
   mind; it depends on what control of the medium does to the conditions of
   thinking. Call the technologically mediated portion of deliberation the
   externalized forum internum.
5. Three traditions of freedom (non-interference, non-domination, conscience)
   converge on cognitive liberty; none alone suffices.
6. Private and sovereign epistemic power are different species of problem;
   the sovereign can universalize a rule and close exits; epistemic exit is
   what open and local models provide, and an exit can be constitutionally
   significant while economically marginal.
7. Historically, control of a cognitive technology has been achieved by
   concentrating its production and enlisting the concentrators (the
   Stationers); regulation has moved from licensing to architecture (Lessig,
   Clipper); and surveillance powers have been abused and then corrected
   (Church).
8. Legibility of persons proceeds by degrees, and AI adds a new degree:
   cognitive legibility, the institutional reconstruction of deliberation
   from traces; its form is modulated permission, not prohibition.
9. The authoritarian archive shows what those affordances do when
   constraints are absent; the lesson is constitutional, not predictive; the
   bottleneck moves from collection to computation.
10. Control of thought through an intermediary works by probability shaping,
    not prohibition; a mutable intermediary threatens the factual world
    Arendt's politics presupposes; the liberal answer is not neutrality about
    truth but suspicion of any final sovereign of it.
11. The hard case is real: a model is not a book; present evidence is sober
    on both sides; the boundary to police is between governing externalized
    capability and governing deliberation, and it runs between layers of the
    machine.
12. A seven-question test locates that boundary; the failure mode is a
    public-private cognitive-security complex issuing in permissioned
    cognition; the alternative is plural; the freedom of the mind must be
    allowed to follow the mind out of the skull.

## The opening

Move: documented miniature, opening variant 5/7. Paragraph one narrates the
first half of the incident from OpenAI's report (dated, attributed, cited
from the first sentence, evidentiary status stated: "by OpenAI's own
account"). Paragraph two narrates the second half from the FT and any
corroboration (attributed: "as the *Financial Times* reports"). Paragraph
three states what neither camp can do with it: four short sentences of the
symmetry, plainly. Paragraph four: the hinge sentence, then the refusal —
"The political debate asks how artificial intelligence should be controlled.
That is the wrong question to ask first" — and the prior question. Paragraph
five: the scope paragraph in the third person ("This essay is about…"), five
parts, each paid off; then "That takes the argument backward four
centuries." Stakes are clear by the end of paragraph three (~450 words); the
thesis arrives in paragraph four (~600 words), within the long form's
attested delay.

Provenance: no invented scene. Every sentence of the incident carries a
citation; nothing is narrated that a fetched source does not state.

## The ending

The coda returns the epigraph: the line's confidence "depended partly on
technology" (four short sentences: the body could be imprisoned; books
burned; speech forbidden; inward reflection remained hard to observe). Then
the strange thing AI does: the mind is moved outward voluntarily because
doing so enlarges it. The paradox as one sentence. Then the two losses of
control, and the litany from *The Constitution of Reason*'s register (no
model, company, state, safety institution, or dissenting individual is
identical with reason; truth exceeds all of them). Last two sentences,
close to the outline's: "Make dangerous power governable without making
thought governable. The mind may extend beyond the skull. The freedom of the
mind must be allowed to follow it." The epigraph's key term (*the freedom of
my mind*) returns in the last sentence, per the corpus habit.

## Apparatus budget

- 1 epigraph; 1 unnumbered opening header; 15 roman-numeral headers; 1
  unnumbered coda header; 1 References header. No `<h3>` sub-sections.
- `[n]` references: 45–60 entries; markers may bundle.
- Block quotes: ≤3 (the thesis-scale definitions are set as prose, not
  block quotes; a block quote only for a passage the essay then interprets —
  candidates: Shklar's "weak and the powerful"; Deleuze's password sentence).
- Lists: 0. The five layers (§XI) and the seven questions (§XII) are runs of
  short paragraphs, each opening with its name.
- Tables: 0. Bold in prose: 0. Italics for coinages at first use only.
- Section-number cross-references inside the essay: permitted (corpus habit).
