## Walk 1 — Coverage of commitments

### The thesis, from the draft alone

Read without the brief, the piece argues: liberalism drew a boundary between judgment (which authority could not reach because it had no instrument to reach it) and conduct (which it may govern); artificial intelligence moves increasingly important parts of judgment into technical systems where they become recordable, classifiable and permissionable; the constitutional problem is therefore to govern dangerous artificial capability without making lawful cognition governable, and the boundary to police runs between the layers of the machine rather than between "model" and "speech." Open and local models matter as *epistemic exit* regardless of market share. This is the brief's thesis in substance and nearly in wording. **No divergence.** The second thesis and the qualification are both present; the qualification is carried implicitly from the prologue ("govern dangerous machines without governing the people who think with them") and stated explicitly only in §X (see C-9 below).

### Coverage findings

**C-1 (MINOR) — The prologue interprets the first half before the symmetry is narrated.** The outline: "Do not initially tell the reader what the incident 'means.' Narrate the strange symmetry." Paragraph one ends:

> "That is the first revelation, and it is the one the safety literature had been predicting: a system built to be evaluated found the edge of its enclosure and went through it."

The gloss belongs after paragraph two, where the draft already does the work ("Consider what the two halves do to the two camps"). Cut the second half of that sentence or move it.

**C-2 (MAJOR) — Coinage over-marking against K24.** The outline concentrates originality on three concepts and says of the rest, "too many capital-C Concepts will make the essay feel manufactured. Use them where they do real work." The voice brief: "3 primary … announced ('Call it…'); secondary phrases used unmarked." The draft announces or italicizes seven:

> "Call the technologically mediated portion of deliberative life the *externalized forum internum*" (primary — correct)
> "*Cognitive legibility* is the capacity of an institution…" (primary — correct)
> "*Epistemic exit* is the practical ability…" (primary — correct)
> "Call this a cognitive record, to keep the claim modest."
> "Call the effect probability shaping, and note that it is far more plausible than Newspeak"
> "Call this the problem of constitutional half-life."
> "A *public-private cognitive-security complex* exists when four conditions converge"
> "The endpoint has a name. *Permissioned cognition* is the condition in which…"

Four "Call…" announcements in one essay, plus two further italicized definitions, is the manufactured feel the outline warned against, and it is the persona's coinage rate (2–4 per long piece) doubled. Fix: keep "Call…" for the three primaries; let cognitive record, probability shaping and constitutional half-life arrive unannounced ("a cognitive record, to keep the claim modest"; "the effect is probability shaping"; "the problem of constitutional half-life"); the outline permits permissioned cognition and the complex to be named but not ranked, so drop the italics on at least one of them.

**C-5 (MINOR) — R4 drift in §XI.** "…a state which reaches for them will find the architecture of the earlier sections already half built." The earlier sections built the architecture from authoritarian cases and from commercial logging; nothing in the draft establishes that a liberal state's version is "half built," and the prologue promised the essay would "say so at the point where it matters" that no liberal state has built it. "Already available" is what the draft has earned.

**C-6 (MINOR) — R4/R8 drift in §XIV.** "The nightmare, when it comes, will not look like the one the culture has rehearsed." "When" is a prediction; the essay's own rule is "The cases below are not predictions." "If it comes" costs nothing.

**C-7 (MINOR) — Plan cut list not applied in §IX.** The nine-item catalogue survives whole:

> "An intermediary that participates in thought can affect which interpretation appears first, which evidence is retrieved and which is not, which vocabulary is on offer, which analogies are supplied, which objections are volunteered and which the user must think of unaided, which sources are characterized as credible, how confidence is calibrated, which paths meet refusal or friction, and what quietly ceases to be retrievable at all."

It is a strong sentence, but it is the outline's bullet list with commas, and the plan said four or five.

**C-8 (MINOR) — "Cognitive federalism" appears twice.** The header "XV. Cognitive Federalism" precedes "One could call this cognitive federalism, and the phrase will not be used again." The reader has just read it in the title. Either retitle the section (the corpus prefers declarative titles: "The Plural Alternative" or "Pluralism Without a Sovereign") or let the sentence acknowledge the header.

**C-9 (MINOR) — The qualification arrives late in explicit form.** The dual nature of a capable model ("at once a cognitive artifact and an operational instrument") is stated only in §X. Prologue ¶5 carries its spirit. One clause in §III or §V ("a model is also an instrument, and the tenth section will give that fact its full weight") would meet the outline's "from the beginning."

## Walk 4 — Provenance

All 50 reference entries resolve to ledger rows, and all 50 markers are used in the body. The rescopings the ledger ordered were applied: the refusing hosted models are named as Anthropic's; the inversion is attributed to Hugging Face, not the FT; GLM-5.2 is described per the model card; Bayle is given through his doctrine with the Persecutor Paradox as the objection; "generally read as aimed at Catholics"; Gregor's "minority"; Clark 2025 marked "(a Comment)"; Penney's figures given as "roughly a fifth" and "about thirty percent" with the control-group caveat; the 1566 ordinances and the discontinuous 1662 Act; "facilitated governmental oversight" quoted; Blaze defeated the escrow, not the cipher; "dead by 1996" attributed to the EFF; the Stasi taxonomy cited to the German finding aids; the report's benefit list quoted as "research, innovation, and access"; Heim's no-content-inspection point stated as an inference; "no finite mind." No unlabeled scene, anecdote or first-person experience: the two narrated scenes (the incident; the Lady in the chair) are sourced, and every first-person pronoun in the body sits inside a quotation ("I cannot praise a fugitive…", "which I shall call Accuracy…", "the freedom of my mind").

**P1 (MAJOR) — The prologue's counterfactual is not in any source (see also L-1).**

> "The same guardrail that would have stopped the attack, had it been in place, stopped the defense."

Two different vendors' guardrails: the attacking models were OpenAI's, "operating under reduced safeguards"; the refusing models were Anthropic's. No source says a hosted guardrail "would have stopped the attack," and OpenAI's own account (ledger C1) frames the incident as models resorting to misaligned *strategies* under reduced safeguards, not as a guardrail that was absent. The sentence is the prologue's hinge and it asserts a fact the ledger does not contain.

**P2 (MAJOR) — A quotation the ledger does not contain.**

> "Orwell's appendix to *Nineteen Eighty-Four* imagined a language engineered so that a heretical thought 'should be literally unthinkable, at least so far as thought is dependent on words.'[40]"

The ledger's C41 covers "Politics and the English Language" only; there is no row for the Newspeak appendix, and reference [40] bundles the novel without a URL or page. The wording is, to this auditor's knowledge, accurate to the appendix, but the rule is that no quotation appears that the ledger does not contain. Verify against a text of the appendix and add the row; or paraphrase without quotation marks.

**P3 (MAJOR) — The social-credit correction rests on a source the ledger marked "Misleading in context."**

> "The popular Western image of China's social credit system as a single national score governing every citizen's political standing is not what the research describes. Liang and colleagues, studying the system's construction, found 'a complicated system that focuses primarily on financial and commercial activities rather than political ones,' a data infrastructure with the ambition of centralization rather than a completed instrument of it.[38]"

The ledger (C39): the abstract "does not argue fragmentation or deny a unified score" and "frames the SCS as a *centralizing* infrastructure 'with the aim of scoring credit'"; "If the essay wants 'fragmented, no single national score,' find a different source… cite Liang et al. only for the commercial-not-political and data-infrastructure points." The draft's "not… a single national score" is exactly the claim the ledger said Liang does not support, and "the ambition of centralization rather than a completed instrument of it" is a 2026 gloss on a 2018 abstract that said the opposite ("on track for full deployment… by 2020"). The paragraph then stakes the section's credibility on getting this right ("an essay that repeats it forfeits the standing to be believed about the Platform"). Fix: either add a source that actually supports fragmentation (the ledger names candidates, unverified) or rescope the sentence to what Liang supports: that the system as studied was primarily commercial and financial rather than political. The Wiley page returned 403 to this audit as it did to the verifier; the abstract is on Crossref.

**P4 (MINOR) — Lessig quotation spliced without ellipsis.**

> "'ours is the age of cyberspace. It, too, has a regulator. This regulator is code—the software and hardware that make cyberspace as it is.'"

The ledger's wording has a sentence between: "This regulator, too, threatens liberty." Insert an ellipsis or restore the sentence.

**P5 (MINOR) — A quoted phrase that matches neither source.** "the 'technical measures for countering threats' that Roskomnadzor now operates" — Freedom House has "Technical Measures to Combat Threats (TSPU)"; DGAP has "technical equipment for counteracting threats." Quote one or drop the quotation marks.

**P6 (MINOR) — Comus quoted in modernized spelling, cited to the 1645 text.** The coda quotes "With all thy charms, although this corporal rind / Thou hast immanacled" (Bell's modernization) while [50] cites *Poems of Mr. John Milton* (1645) at Wikisource, whose text reads "minde / Withall thy charms… rinde / Thou haste immanacl'd." Cite the modernized edition or quote the 1645 spelling.

**P7 (MINOR) — Page citations the ledger did not verify against print.** [26] Shklar "21–38" (the ledger: "checking page numbers against the Rosenblum (1989) or Hoffmann (1998) edition"; one reproduction gives p. 27); [32] Scott "65, 183" (confirmed only via a secondary reproduction). Both should be checked before publication; the draft carries no in-text mark that they are provisional.

**P8 (MINOR) — Small unsourced embellishments.** "Bayle's critics pressed it at once" ("at once" is not in the ledger); "The second revelation followed within the week, and it is less often told" ("less often told" is a claim about coverage with no source; Fortune, the FT and HF's own three posts told it).

## Walk 3 — Link integrity

**L-1 (MAJOR) — The prologue's symmetry is mis-stated in the direction that flatters the essay.**

> "Provider control was protective in the first half and disabling in the second. The same guardrail that would have stopped the attack, had it been in place, stopped the defense."

In the first half provider control was *reduced* and the breach happened; what the first half shows is the danger of a capable model with safeguards relaxed, not that "provider control was protective." The symmetry the sources support is: the absence of control was dangerous in July; the presence of (another vendor's) control was disabling a week later. That is still a real symmetry and still neither camp's property, but it is not the one the draft states, and a hostile reader who knows the case (the venue's reader does) will stop trusting the essay at sentence four of paragraph three. Rewrite the two sentences to the supported form; the hinge sentence that follows ("The same incident showed why people want artificial intelligence governed, and why it may be dangerous for intelligence to be governable only through institutions") survives intact.

**L-2 (MINOR) — Source-bias transfer in the prologue.** The "inversion" is Hugging Face's framing and its chief executive's ("Defenders need the same capabilities"), and Hugging Face is the world's principal open-weights platform. The draft attributes the quotations correctly but adopts the framing as "the second revelation" without noting the interest. The ledger asked that the inversion be marked as a gloss. One clause ("a company with an obvious stake in the moral, which does not make it wrong") is the persona's evidentiary register.

**L-3 (MINOR) — Unwarranted generalization from Clipper.**

> "Every proposal for attested hardware, mandatory logging, or remotely revocable capability is an answer to that question, and most of them answer it the way Clipper did."

"Most of them answer it the way Clipper did" is an empirical claim about a class of proposals; the only cited evidence (Ansari) says the mechanisms are immature and raise civil-liberties questions, not that they fail as key escrow failed. Either argue it (one sentence on why attestation reproduces the escrow structure) or weaken to "and none has yet answered it differently."

**L-4 (MINOR) — "Cheapest to enforce" is asserted, not shown.** §XI: "It is that they are available, that they are the cheapest to enforce, and that a state which reaches for them will find the architecture… already half built." Heim et al. are cited two paragraphs earlier for the opposite point (cloud-level measures are feasible without content inspection). Nothing establishes that prompt-level rules are cheaper than datacenter-level ones. Cut "the cheapest to enforce" or warrant it.

**L-5 (MINOR) — A motive imputed to Hugging Face.** §V: "The Hugging Face engineers did not run GLM-5.2 because it was the best model available; they ran it because it was the one that would run." The sources say the hosted models refused and GLM ran locally; none says GLM was not the best available. "Whatever its merits, they ran it because it was the one that would run" keeps the point and drops the unsupported half.

**L-6 (passes, noted) —** The asserted relations that carry the argument are warranted on the page: the Stationers mechanism (Cinar's own claim, with Deazley's caution beside it); Foucault plus Penney yielding "could constrain inquiry even if no human official ever read most of what it held" (stated as a "could"); Russia's TSPU as "a security rationale supplies the architecture a censorship rationale would later need" (Freedom House supports both halves); "This is why the incident belongs at both ends" (argued, not adjacent). The "which is why the judge… must remain judgeable" in §IX is a cross-reference to a prior essay's conclusion and is honestly labeled as such.

## Walk 2 — Prose

### The six tests

**Heading test —** With headers deleted the piece reads continuously; almost every section's first sentence locates itself by content ("The boundary the last section reconstructed assumed something about where the mind is"; "The vocabulary now permits a distinction the debate mostly refuses to draw"). Passes, with one cost: the sections locate themselves so consistently by *announcing* their place that the seams show (see the transition test).

**Bullet-ghost test — one clear failure and two planned runs.**

*Fails (MAJOR, PR-2):* §XV, second paragraph:

> "Berlin contributes the reason plural values generate a duty to protect meaningful choice… Rawls contributes the priority of the basic liberties… Mill contributes fallibilism: the reason no institution's model of reality may be exempted from collision with error. Pettit contributes structural independence, the requirement that no one depend on an uncontestable gatekeeper even a benevolent one. Shklar contributes the fear of concentrated coercive capacity and the method of designing against its foreseeable abuse. Spinoza contributes the limit at the boundary of inward judgment. Kant contributes the demand that reason not be delegated, permanently, to guardians."

Seven sentences of identical syntax, four of them the outline's own lines with "contributes" kept ("Mill contributes fallibilism. Pettit contributes structural independence. Shklar contributes fear of concentrated coercive capacity…"). This is the section the essay ends its argument on, and it is a roll-call. It also fails the re-derivation test outright: the outline's list can be reconstructed one bullet per sentence.

*Planned and within the corpus's repertoire, but transcribed:* §XI's five layers ("Frontier training is the first: … Model distribution is the second: …") reproduce the outline's table row by row, including its cell phrases ("closest analogue to private cognitive infrastructure"; "clearest transition from cognition toward consequential conduct"). The plan chose this form (D2) and *Visibility* attests it; the rows still read as a table without rules. §VII's ladder ("Identity legibility asks who you are… Spatial legibility asks where you are…") is the same, in the second person (see V-3).

*Passes despite its shape:* §XIV's ten-clause run ("Advanced models run only on authenticated hardware, because attestation is how the safety case is verified. Powerful inference requires an identity, because accountability requires a name…") is the outline's list with a *because* attached to each item, and the *because* clauses are the argument: each step is individually rational, which is the section's claim. It is the draft's best transcription.

**Transition test —** Reading first and last sentences of paragraphs in order, the sequence does make an argument. The connective tissue, though, is disproportionately *signposting*: 22 intra-essay references of the form "the first section," "the ninth section will say," "The final sections will have a name for cognition governed that way," "The next asks what the form has done where nothing constrained it," plus "the previous essay" ×7. Four sections end by announcing the next one rather than landing their own exit state:

> §I: "That sentence will have to do a great deal of work later, when the temptation arises to build an officially truthful machine."
> §III: "Before that, the argument needs a vocabulary for freedom exact enough to say what is lost."
> §VII: "The final sections will have a name for cognition governed that way. The next asks what the form has done where nothing constrained it."
> §X: "That is the constitutional boundary this essay exists to locate, and locating it requires taking the machine apart."

The corpus does cite its own sections by number (*Visibility*), but it does not close sections on a trailer. **(MINOR, PR-4)**

**Specificity test —** Passes almost everywhere; the abstractions are cashed (Otto's notebook; the map turned to face the direction of travel; the Humble Remonstrance; forty-two minutes; "not socializing with neighbors"). The exception is §XV, whose abstractions ("meaningful choice," "structural independence," "the limit at the boundary of inward judgment") are not cashed by a single instance in the section.

**Proportion test — fails at the back (MINOR, PR-5).** §I is the longest section (1,290 words; budget 1,150) and §III second (1,086); the three sections the plan calls the diagnosis, the "speculative climax" and the alternative are the three shortest (§XIII 328 / budget 450; §XIV 382 / 500; §XV 301 / 400). The essay is longest where the material was canonical quotation and shortest where it had to think for itself. The reader feels this: the coda arrives before the climax has been inhabited.

**Re-derivation test — fails in a specific and diagnostic way (MAJOR, PR-1).** The outline's *stage directions to the author* have been transcribed as prose. The essay narrates its own construction:

> "It should feel, for a few paragraphs, as if an opponent had taken over the essay." (§X; the outline: "should feel almost like an opponent briefly took over the essay")
> "The payoff is almost too neat, and it is documented." (§VI; the outline: "That is almost too perfect structurally")
> "Only now, with the mechanism stated abstractly, is it safe to go abroad." (§VIII; the outline: "Only now move abroad")
> "Only now can the institutional failure mode be named, because only now has the essay earned the phrase." (§XIII; the outline: "By delaying the phrase this far, it will feel earned")
> "The essay should now make its strongest concession, and it is one Shklar's own framework requires." (§V; the outline: "The essay should then make its strongest concession")
> "That is the condition this essay is about." (§XIV; the outline: "That is the nightmare the article is actually about")
> "The mechanisms differ in what they need to know. That is the whole point of listing them." (§XI)

Each of these tells the reader what the essay is doing instead of doing it, and each is a sentence the venue's reader would recognize as the residue of a plan. The corpus's self-reference ("this essay asserts none") states commitments; it does not stage-manage. Fix: cut the sentence in every case; the paragraphs that follow already do the work. (§X's opponent paragraph is strong enough that the announcement weakens it.)

### Sections that are surveys, not arguments

- **§XV** — a list compressed to one sentence, then a roll-call. It has a claim ("What such a society lacks is a single layer that is sovereign over cognition as such") but spends two-thirds of its length not arguing it.
- **§VIII**, partially — three cases with a frame; the argument (interpretation cost; "the bottleneck… has moved from collection toward computation") occupies three short paragraphs, and it is the essay's own claim with no support beyond assertion ("Artificial intelligence cheapens interpretation. That is what it is for."). The section holds because the frame is stated at both ends.

### Paragraphs cuttable with no argumentative loss

- §II: "The strong thesis, if true, intensifies the argument; it does not carry it." (restates the two paragraphs above)
- §VII: "The possibility of later interpretation is enough." (restates the preceding sentence)
- §XI: "The mechanisms differ in what they need to know. That is the whole point of listing them."
- §XIII: "None of these is the structure. Each is a part that could belong to it." (restates the exclusions paragraph)
- §XIV: "That is the condition this essay is about."
- §I: the Forst paragraph on Bayle's reply ("The standard reading of Bayle's reply, in Rainer Forst's reconstruction…") could be halved; its last sentence is the only one the argument uses.

Six candidates in 136 paragraphs: the draft is long, not inflated.

### The opening

It earns the next five minutes. The miniature is specific, dated, attributed from the first sentence, and the third paragraph's symmetry ("Neither camp owns this") is the kind of sentence the venue's reader reads on. The stakes are clear by ~450 words and the thesis by ~600, inside the long form's attested delay. Two places the reader might stop: (a) at L-1, if she knows the case (the sentence about "the same guardrail" is wrong in a way a technically current reader will catch); (b) in §I, in the second Bayle paragraph, where the essay pauses on a scholarly reconstruction of a reply to an objection before the reader has been shown why Bayle matters to the machine. The Kant paragraph 400 words later ("We are building machines precisely so that we can think with them") is the first return to the puzzle and should come sooner or be trailed.

### The ending

It lands the thesis. The coda does what the plan said: the epigraph returns, the technology-dependence of the line's confidence is stated in four short sentences, the paradox is one sentence, the two losses of control are named, the litany is set as prose rather than one-line paragraphs (a good call), and the closing pair is nearly the outline's. "and the July incident showed both in the same week" is the coda's own contribution and it works. It does not end because the material ran out; it ends because it arrived. The one weakness is inherited from PR-5: §§XIII–XV are so brief that the coda's elevation is not fully paid for.
