## The piece

- **Title:** *The Governable Mind*
- **Subtitle:** *Artificial Intelligence, Cognitive Liberty, and the
  Constitutional Limits of the Legible Mind*
  (the outline's stated preference over *Permissioned Cognition* and *The
  Externalized Conscience*; the subtitle follows the corpus's long triadic
  form, as in *The Constitution of Reason*)
- **Epigraph:** Milton, *Comus* (1634), "Thou canst not touch the freedom of my
  mind" — pending verification of the exact wording (C3). The epigraph names
  the frame (the inaccessible interior), not the title's obvious thinker
  (Foucault, Orwell), which is the corpus's habit.
- **Thesis, strongest form:** Liberalism was built around a boundary between an
  inner domain of judgment and an outer domain of conduct that political
  authority may govern. Artificial intelligence moves increasingly important
  parts of judgment across that boundary into technical systems, where they
  become recordable, classifiable, and permissionable. The constitutional
  problem of AI is therefore not only how to govern dangerous artificial
  capability but how to do so without making lawful human cognition itself
  governable.
- **Second thesis:** open and locally executable models matter politically as
  *epistemic exit* — the possibility of leaving an institutionally governed
  cognitive system for one under the person's own control — regardless of
  market share.
- **The qualification, built in from the start:** epistemic liberty does not
  imply that every executable capability must be unrestricted; a capable model
  is at once a cognitive artifact and an operational instrument; the task is
  to keep governing dangerous external power distinct from governing the
  deliberation that precedes its exercise.
- **Genre:** long-form philosophical essay, analytic-philosophical register
  with a documented-miniature opening; the history of ideas run forward into
  a contemporary constitutional problem.
- **Success looks like:** a reader who arrived thinking the AI debate was
  "safety versus openness" leaves holding a different question — which layer
  of the machine a rule touches and what it must know to be enforced — and
  regards the three coinages (externalized forum internum, cognitive
  legibility, epistemic exit) as tools rather than slogans.

  self-published; "Philosophy, cultural commentary, the history of ideas."

  "administrative subpoena" with one-clause glosses. Assumed to have read

  obviously necessary and open weights are reckless, or that safety talk is
  incumbent capture. **What the reader will resist:** the piece's refusal of
  both — its concession that the safety case is factually serious *and* its
  claim that the ordinary civil-libertarian framing (models as speech) is
