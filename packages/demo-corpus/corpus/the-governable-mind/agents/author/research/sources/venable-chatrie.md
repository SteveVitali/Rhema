# venable-chatrie
- **URL:** https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/07/supreme-court-holds-geofence-warrant-access · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, item 8
- **What it establishes:** Venable LLP client alert, July 2026. Chatrie v. United States: 6–3, Justice Kagan; acquiring Location History via a geofence warrant is a Fourth Amendment search; Court relied on Carpenter; rejected the government's short-duration and opt-in arguments; left the validity of the warrant (probable cause/particularity) to the Fourth Circuit; Jackson (with Sotomayor) concurred; Gorsuch concurred in judgment (papers/effects); Alito and Barrett dissented. The editor's "not outside the scope of the Fourth Amendment just because the data is held by a third party" line is Venable's gloss, not the Court's words.
- **Key passages:** "The decision establishes that law enforcement access to Location History is not outside the scope of the Fourth Amendment just because the data is held by a third party." / "Fourth Amendment protection cannot turn on whether a user clicked through a particular setting." / "Google has since altered the way that it stores location data, storing the information on individual devices rather than internally by the company."
- **Reliability:** Secondary (law-firm summary); accurate against the slip opinion.
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 8; supplies the practical framing (third-party custody does not remove protection) and the Google data-minimization coda.
