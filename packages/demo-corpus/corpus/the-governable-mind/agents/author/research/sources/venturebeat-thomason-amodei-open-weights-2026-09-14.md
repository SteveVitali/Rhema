- **What it establishes:** James Thomason, "Amodei's AI slowdown plan never says open weights. It doesn't have to.", VentureBeat, "12:59 pm, PT, September 14, 2026". Guest opinion (bio: Managing Partner at Next Wave Partners; he opposed SB 1047 in VentureBeat two years earlier). Argues Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" (Sept 12) checkpoint-certification scheme excludes open weights de facto; invokes Stigler, the Stationers' Company, and Bernstein. Does NOT mention the Kill Switch Act, the Senate draft, or the August White House framework.
- **Key passages:**
  - "That is why Amodei never has to ask Washington to outlaw open weights. A compliance regime that closed models can meet and open models cannot does the same work, and a certification that must travel with the model is exactly that. Publication stays legal in the same sense that owning a tiger stays legal. The paperwork does the hunting."
  - Preceding para: "For Claude, this costs nothing. Claude is under house arrest by design. Every prompt reports to Anthropic's servers ... An open-weight model has no ankle. It is a file."
  - He flags his own reading as an inference: "Accompanied. I read that as a certification the model carries, not one it clears once at the door and leaves behind. If Amodei means the narrower thing, he should say so".
  - Bernstein: "The United States tried to control strong cryptography through export licenses and publication restraints. A district court and a Ninth Circuit panel in the Bernstein case ruled that source code used to communicate scientific ideas received First Amendment protection and that the licensing system imposed an unconstitutional prior restraint." · "Model weights raise distinct legal questions, yet a permission system for computational research carries the same odor of prior restraint."
  - Capture: "George Stigler described the mechanism decades ago. Industries acquire regulation and often operate it for their own benefit." · "Competitors coordinating the pace of output form a cartel. 'Safety' supplies the lab coat."
  - Stationers: "The English Crown licensed printing through the Stationers' Company. The arrangement gave the government censorship and established printers a monopoly."
  - Concession: "Credit where it is due: the first prong costs Anthropic something real."
  - Quotes Amodei, July 27: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models."
- **Reliability:** Secondary opinion; interested (investor, long-time opponent of frontier regulation). "Ninth Circuit panel" is accurate phrasing (the 1999 panel opinion was later withdrawn for en banc rehearing; he does not say so).
