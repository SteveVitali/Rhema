# wikipedia-letter-concerning-toleration
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Letter_Concerning_Toleration · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C7 (attribution and interpretive caveat only)
- **What it establishes:** That the *Letter* was composed in Latin and "published in English translation in 1689 by William Popple, without Locke's knowledge"; and that the Catholic reading of the "another prince" exclusion is standard but debated (Goldie, Sowerby argue Locke left room to tolerate Catholics who renounced political allegiance to the Pope).
- **Key passages:**
  - "originally composed in Latin and was published in English translation in 1689 by William Popple, without Locke's knowledge."
  - Summarized: scholars such as Mark Goldie and Scott Sowerby argue Locke may have allowed toleration of Catholics "if Catholics could discard their uncivil beliefs" or "rejected political allegiance to the Pope."
- **Reliability:** Tertiary; used only for translator attribution and to flag the interpretive debate. Do not cite in the essay.
- **Relevance to this project:** C7 — supports phrasing "read as Catholics" rather than "Locke excluded Catholics."
