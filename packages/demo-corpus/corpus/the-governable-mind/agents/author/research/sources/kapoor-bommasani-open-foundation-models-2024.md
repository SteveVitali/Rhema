# kapoor-bommasani-open-foundation-models-2024
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07918 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07918, v1 27 Feb 2024; 23 pp.) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, open-weights properties / marginal-risk framing
- **What it establishes:** Defines open foundation models as those "with widely available model weights" (fn. 1, p. 1) and articulates five distinctive properties (Sec. 3, p. 3). The paper's own labels are: "Broader access," "Greater customizability," "Local adaptation and inference ability," "Inability to rescind model access," "Inability to monitor or moderate model usage." The Sec. 1 summary compresses these as "broader access, greater customizability, the potential for local inference, an inability to rescind model access once released, and weaker monitoring" (pp. 1–2). Introduces a six-point marginal-risk framework (Sec. 5.1, p. 5). Printed page = PDF page. arXiv lists no journal-ref; it is self-described as a "position paper."
- **Key passages:**
  - Sec. 1, pp. 1–2: "Open foundation models are marked by broader access, greater customizability, the potential for local inference, an inability to rescind model access once released, and weaker monitoring."
  - Sec. 3, p. 3: "Once the weights for a foundation model are made widely available, little recourse exists for the foundation model developer to rescind access."
  - Sec. 3, p. 3: "In all cases, foundation model developers do not observe inference by default, making monitoring or moderation challenging, especially for local inference."
  - Sec. 5, p. 5: "we present a framework that centers marginal risk: what additional risk is society subject to because of open foundation models relative to pre-existing technologies, closed models, or other relevant reference points?"
  - Sec. 5.1, p. 6: "the marginal risk analysis need not only be conducted relative to the status quo, but potentially relative to other (possibly hypothetical) baselines."
  - Abstract: "current research is insufficient to effectively characterize the marginal risk of open foundation models relative to pre-existing technologies."
- **Reliability:** Primary. Large multi-institution author list (Princeton, Stanford, MIT, Meta, Hugging Face, EleutherAI, OSI, etc.); several authors are affiliated with open-model organizations, so the paper is sympathetic to openness, though it is careful and evidence-focused.
- **Relevance to this project:** Supplies the exact wording for the five properties if the essay cites them; note the paper says "weaker monitoring"/"inability to monitor or moderate," not simply "inability to monitor."
