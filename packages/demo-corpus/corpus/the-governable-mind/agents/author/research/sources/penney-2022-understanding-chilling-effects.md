# penney-2022-understanding-chilling-effects
- **URL:** https://digitalcommons.osgoode.yorku.ca/scholarly_works/3080/ (abstract page; full PDF listed but not opened) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, repair material on chilling effects
- **Citation:** Jonathon W. Penney, "Understanding Chilling Effects," 106 *Minn. L. Rev.* 1451 (2022).
- **What it establishes:** Penney's abstract states the critique of fear/deterrence-based chilling-effects theory and the alternative social-conformity account, verbatim as the editor summarized.
- **Key passages (abstract):**
  - "I argue that conventional theories, based on fear of legal or privacy harm, are narrow, are empirically weak, cannot predict or explain chilling effects in a range of different contexts, and neglect the productive dimensions of chilling effects—how chilling effects shape behavior."
  - "Drawing extensively on social science literature, I argue that chilling effects are best understood as a form of social conformity. Chilling effects arise out of contexts of ambiguity and uncertainty—like the ambiguity of a va[gue law]…" where people conform to what they take to be the relevant social norms.
- **Reliability:** Primary (author's abstract on institutional repository).
- **Relevance to this project:** If the essay argues that observation of the externalized forum internum chills thinking, Penney warns that the fear-based mechanism is empirically weak; the stronger claim is conformity — thinking-in-a-watched-medium drifts toward the norm the watcher is imagined to hold.
