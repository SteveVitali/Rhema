- **What it establishes:** Bernard Williams, *Truth and Truthfulness: An Essay in Genealogy* (Princeton University Press, 2002). Williams's own definition of the two basic virtues of truth appears on p. 11 (ch. 1) per Google Books snippet, and the term "the two basic virtues of truth" recurs at p. 44 (ch. 3). The NDPR sentence "Accuracy is the virtue of carefully investigating..." is the reviewer's paraphrase, not Williams's words.
- **Key passages:** Williams, p. 11: "basic virtues of truth, which I shall call Accuracy and Sincerity: you do the best you can to acquire true beliefs, and what you say reveals what you believe." · Publisher's description (front matter): "the two basic virtues of truth, Accuracy and Sincerity, the first of which aims at finding out the truth and the second at telling it." · SEP: Williams argues "that it is indispensable to any human society to accept both truth and truthfulness as values, and sincerity and accuracy as corresponding virtues."
- **Reliability:** Primary wording via publisher-authorized search-inside snippets; SEP and NDPR are independent secondary confirmations.
- **Relevance to this project:** C43 — Accuracy and Sincerity as the two virtues of truth.
