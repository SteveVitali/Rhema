# wikipedia-cognitive-liberty
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_liberty · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, finding (c) "Sententia/Boire should be cited" (coinage)
- **What it establishes:** Tertiary confirmation of the CCLE definition, Sententia's two principles (2004), and the scholars' lineage (Blitz 2010; Bublitz & Merkel 2014; Farahany 2012). Sententia 2004 = Ann. N.Y. Acad. Sci. 1013:221–228 (Wikipedia misprints the volume as 13(1); PubMed confirms 1013).
- **Key passages:**
  - CCLE definition: "the right of each individual to think independently and autonomously, to use the full power of his or her mind, and to engage in multiple modes of thought."
  - Sententia 2004, two principles (as paraphrased by Wikipedia): (1) individuals should not be compelled to use brain-interactive technologies or psychoactive drugs if they do not endanger others; (2) individuals should not be prohibited from using mind-altering or mind-enhancing drugs and technologies, provided use does not harm others.
  - Lineage: Blitz, "Freedom of Thought for the Extended Mind," Wis. L. Rev. 2010; Bublitz & Merkel, "Crimes Against Minds," Crim. L. & Phil. 8 (2014); Farahany, "Incriminating Thoughts," 64 Stan. L. Rev. (2012).
- **Reliability:** Tertiary; use only for orientation. The Boire "quintessence of freedom" quotation was NOT verified from the Wikipedia page fetch; it is verified via Bublitz 2013 (draft p. 11), who quotes Boire 2000, p. 8 — see bublitz note.
- **Relevance to this project:** Establishes that "cognitive liberty" as coined by Boire/Sententia (CCLE, 2000–2004) is centrally about the freedom to *use or refuse* mind-altering technologies — a self-determination right — not a privacy-of-record right.
