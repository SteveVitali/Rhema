# risko-gilbert-2016-cognitive-offloading
- **URL:** https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002&resultType=core&format=json (Europe PMC record for PMID 27542527) and https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002 · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C11
- **What it establishes:** Evan F. Risko & Sam J. Gilbert, "Cognitive Offloading," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20(9): 676–688 (September 2016), doi 10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002, PMID 27542527. The paper exists and its abstract gives the canonical one-sentence definition. Full text is paywalled at Cell Press; I read the abstract via Europe PMC and the bibliographic record via Crossref, not the body.
- **Key passages:** "If you have ever tilted your head to perceive a rotated image, or programmed a smartphone to remind you of an upcoming appointment, you have engaged in cognitive offloading: the use of physical action to alter the information processing requirements of a task so as to reduce cognitive demand." / "We offer a novel metacognitive framework that integrates results from diverse domains and suggests avenues for future research."
- **Reliability:** Primary (peer-reviewed review article in a leading cognitive-science journal); abstract text taken from the indexing record, which reproduces the publisher's abstract. No independence concerns.
- **Relevance to this project:** C11; the "offloading" section. The definition is deliberately about *physical action* (head-tilting, setting reminders) reducing cognitive demand; it is not itself a claim about AI or about harm.
