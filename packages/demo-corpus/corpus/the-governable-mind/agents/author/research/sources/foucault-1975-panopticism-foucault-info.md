- **What it establishes:** Full text of "Panopticism," *Discipline & Punish: The Birth of the Prison*, trans. Alan Sheridan (Vintage Books, 1995), pp. 195–228, as hosted by foucault.info. Confirms the exact wording of "visible and unverifiable," "the principle of his own subjection," and the discontinuous-action/permanent-effect point.
- **Key passages (Sheridan trans.):** "Bentham laid down the principle that power should be visible and unverifiable. Visible: the inmate will constantly have before his eyes the tall outline of the central tower from which he is spied upon. Unverifiable: the inmate must never know whether he is being looked at at any one moment; but he must be sure that he may always be so." · "Hence the major effect of the Panopticon: to induce in the inmate a state of conscious and permanent visibility that assures the automatic functioning of power. So to arrange things that the surveillance is permanent in its effects, even if it is discontinuous in its action; that the perfection of power should tend to render its actual exercise unnecessary" · "He who is subjected to a field of visibility, and who knows it, assumes responsibility for the constraints of power; he makes them play spontaneously upon himself; he inscribes in himself the power relation in which he simultaneously plays both roles; he becomes the principle of his own subjection."
- **Reliability:** Primary text (standard translation) on a long-standing Foucault archive site; not the publisher, but the wording matches the Vintage edition it cites.
- **Relevance to this project:** C34 — surveillance that works whether or not anyone watches.
