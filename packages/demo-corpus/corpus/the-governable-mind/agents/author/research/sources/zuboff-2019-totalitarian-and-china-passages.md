- **Citation:** Shoshana Zuboff, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (New York: PublicAffairs; London: Profile Books, 2019), ch. 12 "Two Species of Power" and ch. 13 "Big Other and the Rise of Instrumentarian Power," §IV "The China Syndrome."
- **What it establishes:**
  - **(a) CORRECTION.** Whitehead (PDF p. 13) prints: "Totalitarian power cannot be achieved by remote control. Mere conformity is insufficient. Each inner life must be claimed and transformed by the perpetual threat of punishment without crime" (p.359). But Zuboff's own text, per the Google Books snippet of the PublicAffairs edition AND the full-text scan, reads: "**Totalitarian power cannot succeed by remote control. Mere conformity is insufficient. Each individual inner life must be claimed and transformed by the perpetual threat of punishment without crime.**" Whitehead misquotes twice ("be achieved" for "succeed"; drops "individual"). Google Books returns no Zuboff hit for "cannot be achieved by remote control." The sentence sits in ch. 12 (totalitarianism section, following the Lefort "People-as-one" quotation). Page: p. 359 per Whitehead (Profile edition) — page not independently seen; if the essay quotes, use Zuboff's wording and cite ch. 12 (p. 359 on Whitehead's authority, flagged) or check print.
  - **(b) China.** Ch. 13, §IV "The China Syndrome" (wording from the scan; the two sentences marked † also confirmed in Google Books snippets of the PublicAffairs e-book):
    - "It is now possible to imagine one logical conclusion of this trend toward the substitution of certainty for society as the Chinese government develops a comprehensive 'social credit' system".
    - "Although China's social credit vision is invariably described as 'digital totalitarianism' and is often compared to the world of Orwell's 1984, it is better understood as the apotheosis of instrumentarian power fed by public and private data sources and controlled by an authoritarian state." (phrase "apotheosis of instrumentarian power fed by public and private data sources and controlled by an authoritarian state" also seen quoted, attributed to Zuboff, in a 2024 Google Books snippet.)
    - "The result is an emergent system that allows us to peer into one version of a future defined by a comprehensive fusion of instrumentarian and state power." †("fusion of instrumentarian and state power" confirmed)
    - †"In the Chinese context, the state will run the show and own it, not as a market project but as a political one, a machine solution that shapes a new society of automated behavior for guaranteed political and social outcomes: certainty without terror." … "The state will assume the role of the behaviorist god, owning the shadow text and determining the schedule of reinforcements".
    - Print page numbers for §IV NOT verified (e-book/scan lack them). Cite as "ch. 13, §IV ('The China Syndrome')" unless print is checked.
  - Evangelista (Surveillance & Society 17(1/2): 246–251, at 249; dated 2018 in its own header, © 2019), secondary confirmation: "she identifies China's use of instrumentarian power for the constitution of a 'social credit.' … at home Big Other serves in the creation of profits; in China, it serves a government that wishes to control the social body." Evangelista gives no page/chapter cite.
  - Whitehead p. 13 also: "digital totalitarianism (such as that which appears to be emerging in certain parts of China)" — Whitehead's gloss, which is the label Zuboff *rejects* for social credit.
