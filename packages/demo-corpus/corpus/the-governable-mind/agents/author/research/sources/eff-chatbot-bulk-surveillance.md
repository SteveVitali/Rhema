# eff-chatbot-bulk-surveillance
- **URL:** https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/ai-chatbot-companies-should-protect-your-conversations-bulk-surveillance · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, item 10
- **What it establishes:** EFF Deeplinks, Dec. 2, 2025 (intern Alexandra Halbeck contributed). Argues chat logs are content requiring a warrant (letters, emails, search-engine prompts precedents); warns of "reverse" warrants (tower dumps, geofence, keyword) migrating to chatbots; notes OpenAI's law-enforcement policy acknowledges the warrant requirement while Anthropic's "could stand to be more precise"; asks companies to (1) fight bulk orders, (2) give users advance notice, (3) publish transparency reports including bulk-order counts. Cites Pfefferkorn's post and OpenAI's 2025H1 report.
- **Key passages:** "Before the government can rifle through your private thoughts stored on digital platforms, it must do what it has always been required to do: get a warrant." / "A single chat thread can expose the kind of intimate detail once locked away in a handwritten diary." / "if a company stores a lot of data about its users, law enforcement (and private litigants) will eventually seek it out."
- **Reliability:** Secondary advocacy (civil-liberties NGO); positions are normative, factual references are linked.
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 10; the "private thoughts stored on digital platforms" line and the diary analogy.
