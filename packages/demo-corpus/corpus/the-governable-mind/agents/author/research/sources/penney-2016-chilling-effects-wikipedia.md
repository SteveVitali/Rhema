# penney-2016-chilling-effects-wikipedia
- **URL:** https://btlj.org/data/articles2016/vol31/31_1/0117_0182_Penney_ChillingEffects_WEB.pdf (journal PDF, 66 pp.) and https://digitalcommons.schulichlaw.dal.ca/scholarly_works/268/ (author's repository record) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C22
- **What it establishes:** Jonathon W. Penney, "Chilling Effects: Online Surveillance and Wikipedia Use," 31 *Berkeley Technology Law Journal* 117 (2016), doi 10.15779/Z38SS13. Interrupted time-series on monthly views of 48 English-Wikipedia articles matching DHS "terrorism" keywords, 32 months spanning the June 2013 Snowden/PRISM revelations. Findings, with page numbers: mean monthly views fell 526,614, "approximately a 19.5% drop" (p. 146); the first regression model shows an immediate drop of 995,085 views, "over 30%" of the May 2013 level of 2,960,778, with *no* significant change in secular trend in that model (p. 147); refined models with comparator groups find that, after netting out a ~15% background dip in all-Wikipedia traffic, the terrorism articles "dropped off an additional 11% in June 2013" and showed "a statistically significant negative trend change (38,160 fewer views a month)" while a domestic-security comparator group showed no significant trend change (p. 159). The abstract claims both an immediate decline and a change in the overall secular trend. The much-repeated "nearly 30%" figure in press coverage corresponds to the model-based immediate drop, not the 19.5% mean difference.
- **Key passages:** Abstract (p. 117): "The Article finds not only a statistically significant immediate decline in traffic for these Wikipedia articles after June 2013, but also a change in the overall secular trend in the view count traffic, suggesting not only immediate but also long-term chilling effects". p. 146: "a reduction of 526,614 in the average monthly views for the articles, which represents approximately a 19.5% drop in article view counts." p. 147: "there was a reduction of 995,085 views immediately following the June 2013 revelations ... The total article views as of May 2013 was 2,960,778, meaning this decline represents an immediate drop-off of over 30% of overall views." p. 159: "The terrorism-related articles, consistent with a chilling effect hypothesis, dropped off an additional 11% in June 2013." / "a statistically significant negative trend change (38,160 fewer views a month)". Limitations acknowledged (p. 145): "although a true control group was not possible, comparator groups were also included in the analysis".
- **Reliability:** Primary, peer-edited law review; single-author observational study with no true control group (author says so). Page-view counts, not unique users. Widely cited, including by A/76/380 para. 54 fn 102.
- **Relevance to this project:** C22 and C17. Give one figure and say which: "roughly a fifth fewer average monthly views" (19.5%) or "an immediate drop of about 30% in the regression model." Do not write "nearly 30% decline in views" without qualification.
- **Addendum 2026-09-16 (slice M / M6) — p. 164 verbatim (journal PDF p. 48):** "The findings here are consistent with Solove's approach to chilling effects. On his account, people censor themselves and avoid certain activities not necessarily out of fear of prosecution but out of concern for potential future harms due to privacy violations, embarrassing public disclosures, risks of fraud or identity theft, or being labeled a criminal, deviant, or non-conformist by state authorities. Given the lack of evidence of people being prosecuted or punished for accessing information on Wikipedia or similar sites before, during, or after the June 2013 revelations, it is unlikely that actual fear of prosecution can fully explain the chilling effects suggested by the findings of this study. Rather, Solove's notion of surveillance-related "pollution"—the broader societal context of self-censorship and conformity arising from ubiquitous and large-scale surveillance—may be the better explanation." (fnn. 156–157 omitted.) Next sentence: "This inference is supported by the independent privacy evaluation completed by the 415 respondents recruited through MTurk." Note the attribution: the "better explanation" is *Solove's "pollution"* as Penney glosses it; hedged "may be".
