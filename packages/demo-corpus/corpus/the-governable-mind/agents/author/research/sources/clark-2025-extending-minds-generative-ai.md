# clark-2025-extending-minds-generative-ai
- **URL:** https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12089268/fullTextXML (full text, open access) and https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9 ; publisher page https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59906-9 (redirect loop, not read) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C14
- **What it establishes:** Andy Clark, "Extending Minds with Generative AI," *Nature Communications* 16, article 4627 (published 19 May 2025), doi 10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9, PMID 40389440, PMCID PMC12089268, CC BY-NC-ND. Article type is **Comment** (a short opinion piece, ~4 pp.), not a research article. It argues that humans "are and always have been" extended minds/hybrid thinking systems, places generative AI in a lineage running from writing (Plato's *Phaedrus*) through pen-and-paper, GPS and search, and urges caution about "click-rate maximizing algorithms" while rejecting the "techno-gloom" framing.
- **Key passages:** Standfirst: "As human-AI collaborations become the norm, we should remind ourselves that it is our basic nature to build hybrid thinking systems – ones that fluidly incorporate non-biological resources." Body: "we humans are and always have been, what New York University philosopher David Chalmers and I call 'extended minds' – hybrid thinking systems defined (and constantly re-defined) across a rich mosaic of resources only some of which are housed in the biological brain". "In Plato's Phaedrus, written around 370BC, we find a clear statement of the fear that new-fangled inventions such as reading and writing will have catastrophic effects on human memory." "We have not achieved this by becoming dumber and dumber brains but by becoming smarter and smarter hybrid thinking systems." "To those old ploys we now add the burgeoning realms of VR, AR, AIs, apps for everything, and semi-intelligent devices." On risk: "We might become content-curators rather than creators, and the passive targets of click-rate maximizing algorithms that do not have our best interests at heart." "There is ample reason to be alert and cautious".
- **Reliability:** Primary, open-access full text via PMC mirror. Author is the co-originator of the extended-mind thesis, so it is an advocate's statement, not a neutral survey.
- **Relevance to this project:** C14; the "AI as the newest notebook" move and the counter-worry about algorithms that "do not have our best interests at heart" — both are in this one piece.
