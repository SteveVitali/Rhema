# pfefferkorn-chatgpt-warrant
- **URL:** https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2025/10/eight-or-so-questions-to-ask-about-the-chatgpt-warrant/ · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, item 3
- **What it establishes:** Riana Pfefferkorn (Stanford CIS), Oct. 24/25, 2025. Reports Forbes (Thomas Brewster, Oct. 20, 2025) on "the first known federal search warrant asking OpenAI for user data," D. Me., a long-running HSI child-exploitation investigation; an undercover HSI agent chatted with the site admin, who described his ChatGPT prompts and responses; the warrant sought account details, other conversations, names, addresses, payment data; OpenAI complied with an Excel spreadsheet. She coins "reverse prompt warrants," notes this one was properly scoped to one user, and lists eight questions (judge-shopping, OpenAI's capability to search by prompt, gag orders, technical changes to make compliance impossible). Also notes OpenAI's 2H2024 transparency report: 71 requests.
- **Key passages:** "User data is 'if you keep it, they will come': If a tech company stores data about its users, in a form the company can legibly access ... it will receive a government request for some user's data eventually". / "let's call them 'reverse prompt warrants'". / "The affidavit includes two 'unique, specific' prompts and the 'unique responses' that ChatGPT generated." / "this warrant looks properly scoped to seek information about one specific ChatGPT user".
- **Reliability:** Secondary expert commentary (surveillance-law scholar), relying on Forbes and the CourtListener affidavit (linked). Independent.
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 3; the "identified by the content of his prompts" mechanism; the "if you keep it, they will come" line for the essay's legibility thesis.
