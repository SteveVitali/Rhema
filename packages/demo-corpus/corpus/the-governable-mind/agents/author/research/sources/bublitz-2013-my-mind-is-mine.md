# bublitz-2013-my-mind-is-mine
- **URL:** https://www.antoniocasella.eu/dnlaw/Bublitz_2013.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, finding (b) "Bublitz rejects Blitz's move by name"
- **Citation:** Jan Christoph Bublitz, "My Mind is Mine!? Cognitive Liberty as a Legal Concept," in E. Hildt & A. Franke (eds.), *Cognitive Enhancement* (Springer 2013), ch. 19, pp. 233–264. (The file is the author's DRAFT, headed "This is a draft version. Please cite the original"; page numbers below are draft pages 1–33. Published pagination not verified.)
- **What it establishes:** All four editor claims verified verbatim. (1) Footnote 23 names Blitz (2010) and rejects the extended-mind expansion of freedom of thought on the ground that it collapses personality rights into property rights. (2) Freedom of thought "is an absolute right, i.e. there are no limitation clauses" and therefore "has to be construed narrowly." (3) The term "cognitive liberty" is attributed to "Boire 2000; Sententia 2004; Blitz 2010." (4) Cognitive liberty = "a right to mental self-determination" = "sovereignty over their minds." Note: the fn 23 objection is a *property vs. personality* objection to protecting devices *as thought*; it is not an argument that records of thought held on devices should be readable — it says their protection "is based on property rights."
- **Key passages:**
  - draft p. 9 (§X.3): "Cognitive liberty or a right to mental self-determination guarantees individuals sovereignty over their minds. As said, such a right is not enshrined in constitutions, human-rights treaties or legal textbooks."
  - draft p. 11: "The term cognitive liberty has only recently been put forward by US legal scholars and civil rights activists (e.g. Boire 2000; Sententia 2004; Blitz 2010). As Boire correctly observes: The right to control one's own consciousness is the quintessence of freedom. If freedom is to mean anything, it must mean that each person has an inviolable right to think for him or herself." (Boire 2000, p. 8)
  - draft p. 13 (§X.4.1): "Freedom of thought is an absolute right, i.e. there are no limitation clauses allowing restrictions. Whatever falls within the ambit of freedom of thought is hence off-limits for state regulations." fn 11: "E.g. UN General Comment No. 22, 1993: Art. 18 UDHR does 'not permit any violation whatsoever on the freedom of thought.'" Also: "Most agree that freedom of thought protects the forum internum, understood as a person's inner sphere in which opinions and thoughts are formed and revised (in contrast to the outward manifestation of beliefs in the forum externum…)."
  - draft p. 14: "In light of its absolute (unrestrictable) nature, freedom of thought has to be construed narrowly, but, I suggest, not void of any practical application. The right has to guarantee basic capacities required for performing mental acts such as thinking, rational reflection or revision of arguments and has to protect against manipulations."
  - draft p. 21: "In this formulation, the right is a liberty to mental self-determination."
  - draft p. 23, fn 23 (verbatim): "Blitz (2010) puts forward a different claim. Drawing on the extended mind thesis by Clark and Chalmers, he proposes that the protection of freedom of thought should be expanded to 'activity that is […] the functional equivalent of thought' and therewith to computers, IPhones and other devices. However, this expansion eliminates the distinction between personality and property rights. While technical devices / data-storage need (and in fact, enjoy) legal protection, their protection is based on property rights. Even though machines might be functionally similar, freedom of thought can only be meaningfully construed in relation to the human mental realm."
  - References (draft p. 30, 33): "Boire RG (2000) On Cognitive Liberty, Part I. Journal of Cognitive Liberties 1:7-13." "Sententia W (2004) Cognitive Liberty and Converging Technologies for Improving Human Cognition. Annals of the N.Y Academy of Sciences 1013:221-[228]."
- **Reliability:** Primary (author's draft of a published chapter). Bublitz is the leading continental theorist of the right; his position is a known counterweight to Blitz.
- **Relevance to this project:** The essay must confront fn 23 head-on: Bublitz's objection is that device-protection is *property* protection. The essay's reply can be that the objection addresses the wrong right — the essay claims a *privacy-of-thought / non-disclosure* protection for the record, not that the device thinks. Also supplies the exact "no limitation clauses … construed narrowly" framing the essay opens from.
