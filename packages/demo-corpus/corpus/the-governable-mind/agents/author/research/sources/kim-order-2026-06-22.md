# kim-order-2026-06-22
- **URL:** https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.647496/gov.uscourts.nysd.647496.56.0.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 (2 pp., read in full via a reader proxy) · **For:** v1 response round, item 7
- **What it establishes:** United States v. Kim, 25 Crim. 359 (LGS), Dkt. 56, Order of Judge Lorna G. Schofield dated June 22, 2026 (filed 06/22/26). Denies Kim's motion to quash the SCA warrant served on OpenAI (issued by Magistrate Judge Lehrburger) and dissolves the June 15 directive that OpenAI not respond. Reasoning: § 2703(d), (h)(2)(A) allow only a provider to move to quash, in limited circumstances; "The statute does not authorize a customer or subscriber to move to quash a warrant"; no general pre-execution right (Grubbs); district courts have "uniformly rejected pre-execution challenges to SCA warrants by the customers or subscribers whose information is sought" (quoting Google LLC v. United States, 2025 WL 778150, at *5). The order does not reach privilege or overbreadth. Reuters reported it June 23 ("on Monday").
- **Key passages:** p. 1: "The statute does not authorize a customer or subscriber to move to quash a warrant." / p. 1: "there is no general right to quash warrants pre-execution." / p. 2: "ORDERED that the portion of the June 15, 2026, Order directing OpenAI not to respond to the warrant is DISSOLVED."
- **Reliability:** Primary (court order).
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 7. Corrects the editor's "~June 23" to June 22 (order date), and clarifies the denial was procedural (no standing to quash), not a ruling on privilege.
