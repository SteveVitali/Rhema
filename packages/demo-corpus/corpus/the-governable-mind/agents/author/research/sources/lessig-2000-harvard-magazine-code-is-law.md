# lessig-2000-harvard-magazine-code-is-law
- **URL:** https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2000/01/code-is-law-html · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C30
- **What it establishes:** Lawrence Lessig, Harvard Magazine, dated January 1, 2000 (Jan–Feb 2000 issue). Online headline: 'Lawrence Lessig on the increasing regulation of cyberspace', subhead 'On liberty in cyberspace'; URL slug 'code-is-law'. The essay names code/architecture as cyberspace's regulator and, in its opening, lists the regulators of earlier ages (government, social norms, the market) — but it does NOT state the four-modalities framework as such. The phrase 'code is law' does not appear verbatim in the body.
- **Key passages:** Opening: "Every age has its potential regulator, its threat to liberty. Our founders feared a newly empowered federal government; the Constitution is written against that fear. John Stuart Mill worried about the regulation by social norms in nineteenth-century England". "Many of the progressives in the twentieth century worried about the injustices of the market." "Ours is the age of cyberspace. It, too, has a regulator. This regulator, too, threatens liberty." "This regulator is code--the software and hardware that make cyberspace as it is. This code, or architecture, sets the terms on which life in cyberspace is experienced." "The law in cyberspace--code--will displace them." "We should interrogate the architecture of cyberspace as we interrogate the code of Congress."
- **Reliability:** Primary (the author's own essay); Harvard Magazine is a reputable alumni publication. Popular condensation of Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace (1999).
- **Relevance to this project:** C30: quote the 'This regulator is code' passage; attribute the explicit law/norms/market/architecture four-modalities model to the 1999 book, not this essay.
