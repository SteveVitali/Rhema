# cinar-2025-jiplp-technology-regulation-history
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/jiplp/article/20/10/681/8246693 (DOI 10.1093/jiplp/jpaf054) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claims C28
- **What it establishes:** Ali Ekber Cinar, "Understanding technology regulation through history: insights from the legal history of the printing press and copyright in early modern England," Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice 20(10), Oct 2025, pp. 681–691. Open access (CC-BY). Exists; single author; argues that the Crown–Stationers bargain traded censorship duties for a printing monopoly and that centralising the trade in London made it easier to oversee.
- **Key passages:** (1) Charter: "granted Stationers' Company liverymen a perpetual monopoly over printing, bookselling and bookbinding in London, extended their policing powers". (2) 1566: "On 24 June 1566, the Privy Council, upon the High Commission's request, issued ordinances prohibiting the sale or binding of illegal books and granting extended search rights to the Stationers' Company." (3) Bargain: "In exchange for relieving the state of censorship responsibilities, leading members of the Stationers' Company were granted a near-monopoly over English book printing." (4) "Centralization of the book trade in London, instead of a dispersed industry, facilitated governmental oversight". (5) 1643 Order "mandated licensing and registration of all books with the Company and prohibited their printing without the owner's consent after licensing". (6) 1662 Act "mandated licensing for printing, restricted the number of master printers and presses, limited book imports, reaffirmed the Stationers' Company's quasi-monopoly". (7) 1695: "systematic pre-publication state censorship ceased in England, which also ended the state-printer alliance on censorship and enforcement".
- **Reliability:** Secondary; peer-reviewed law journal (OUP); single-author synthesis of standard book-trade history (Blagden, Siebert, Clegg, Deazley). Frames history through a 'technology regulation' lens that fits the essay's thesis — beware confirmation.
- **Relevance to this project:** C28 backbone. Note Cinar dates the *extended* search rights to 1566 ordinances, not the 1557 charter itself; and 1695 quote is the cleanest line for the 'alliance ended' beat.
