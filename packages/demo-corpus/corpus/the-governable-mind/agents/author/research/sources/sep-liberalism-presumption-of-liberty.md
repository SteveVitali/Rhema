# sep-liberalism-presumption-of-liberty
- **URL:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/ · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, ledger C68 (the presumption in favour of liberty, uncited)
- **What it establishes:** SEP "Liberalism" (Shane D. Courtland, Gerald Gaus, David Schmidtz; substantive revision Feb. 25, 2026), §1.1 "The Presumption in Favor of Liberty": liberalism starts from the premise that political authority and legal constraint must be justified; the burden lies with those who would restrict.
- **Key passages:** "Liberalism is a philosophy that starts from a premise that political authority must be justified. Legal constraints must be justified." · Mill, as quoted in the entry: "the burden of proof is supposed to be with those who are against liberty; who contend for any restriction or prohibition" (Mill 1963, vol. 21: 262).
- **Reliability:** Secondary, peer-reviewed reference work.
- **Relevance to this project:** Citation for §IV's "contemporary liberal accounts keep the presumption."
