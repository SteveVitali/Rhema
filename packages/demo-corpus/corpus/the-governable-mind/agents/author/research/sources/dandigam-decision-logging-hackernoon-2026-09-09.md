# dandigam-decision-logging-hackernoon-2026-09-09
- **URL:** https://hackernoon.com/decision-logging-the-observability-pattern-that-actually-helps-ai-agents · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 (direct curl, 200) · **For:** v2→v3 response round, slice N (item N2)
- **What it establishes:** A practitioner article (Raju Dandigam, Staff Software Engineer at Navan; HackerNoon, September 9th, 2026) recommending structured "decision" events instead of prompt/completion capture. Its AgentDecision schema has NO user field at all (decisionId, traceId, spanId, runId, agent, kind, occurredAt, reasonCode, reasonSummary, inputFacts, proposedTools, executedTools, confidence, costUsd, latencyMs, status). A user identifier is treated as optional and, if needed, pseudonymised with a keyed HMAC.
- **Key passages:**
  - Under "Pseudonymous Is Not Anonymous": "Decision logging can become a privacy risk if identifiers are handled casually. Do not record raw prompts, full completions, email addresses, access tokens, payment details, or private chat history by default."
  - "If you need a stable pseudonymous identifier, use a keyed HMAC rather than a plain unsalted hash or a public salt" (followed by a pseudonymizeUserId function); "the result is still pseudonymous data. Access controls, key rotation, purpose limitation, and retention rules still apply."
  - "Do not ask a model to dump its private reasoning into logs and treat that text as ground truth. Free-form rationale may expose sensitive content, be unfaithful to the behavior, and create a much larger security surface. Log the evidence and policy rule the application actually used."
  - Under "Log the Boundary, Not the Entire Runtime": the anti-pattern is logging {user, prompt, toolResults, modelResponse}; "In production, it can copy personal data, secrets, proprietary documents, and long model content into an observability system."
  - "collect the minimum evidence needed for a defined debugging or measurement purpose."
- **Reliability:** Single practitioner's how-to on a self-publishing platform; illustrative of practice, not authoritative. Purpose is debugging/observability, not accountability or attribution — which is why it can drop the user.
- **Relevance to this project:** Answer to "do both keep a user ID?": WorkOS yes (mandatory, with email in the example); Dandigam no (absent from the schema; optional HMAC pseudonym). The two sources together show the action/prompt separation is real practice, and that whether the actor is identified depends on whether the log's purpose is accountability (WorkOS) or debugging (Dandigam).
