# ala-state-library-privacy-laws
- **URL:** https://www.ala.org/advocacy/privacy/statelaws · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2→v3 response round, slice M (M4)
- **Citation:** American Library Association, "State Privacy Laws Regarding Library Records" (page footer: "Updated November 2021").
- **What it establishes:** The 48-states sentence is verbatim. "Online search histories" and "database search records" appear on the page — but in ALA's *recommended library policy* language, not in any statute or model statute quoted there. The page then lists each state's citation (Kentucky and Hawaii by AG opinion).
- **Key passages:**
  - "Forty-eight states and the District of Columbia have laws protecting the confidentiality of library records. Two states, Kentucky and Hawaii, have attorney generals' opinions protecting library users' privacy. The language of these provisions vary from state to state. The majority of these laws declare that a library user's records and information are confidential, and not subject to disclosure, unless certain conditions are met, such as a user's consent or the service of a court order."
  - "The policy should also recognize the confidentiality of any records or electronic data that disclose the materials consulted, borrowed, or acquired by a library user. Materials subject to privacy and confidentiality restrictions may include online search histories, database search records, ILS records or other circulation records, interlibrary loan records, and all other personally identifiable uses of library materials, facilities, programs or services, such as reference interviews."
- **Reliability:** Primary for ALA's count and recommendation; advocacy body; last updated Nov. 2021 (count not independently re-verified for 2026).
- **Relevance to this project:** §III/§V precedent for qualified, court-order-gated protection of records of intellectual activity. Do not write that the statutes cover search histories; write that ALA recommends library policies treat them as covered. Most statutes are qualified (consent/court order) — a model for "rationale, not unlimited scope."
