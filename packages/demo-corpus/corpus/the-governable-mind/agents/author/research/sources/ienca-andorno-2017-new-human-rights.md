- **What it establishes:** Marcello Ienca & Roberto Andorno, "Towards new human rights in the age of neuroscience and neurotechnology," *Life Sciences, Society and Policy* 13, article 5 (26 April 2017), doi 10.1186/s40504-017-0050-1, PMID 28444626, PMCID PMC5447561, CC BY. Proposes exactly the four rights named in the outline.
- **Key passages:** Abstract: "we identify four new rights that may become of great relevance in the coming decades: the right to cognitive liberty, the right to mental privacy, the right to mental integrity, and the right to psychological continuity." Body: "The right to mental privacy is a neuro-specific privacy right which protects private or sensitive information in a person's mind from unauthorized collection, storage, use, or even deletion — in digital form or otherwise." / "the right to mental privacy stands to protect information prior to any extra-cranial externalization (e.g. in verbal or printed format) as well as the generator of such information (a person's neural processing)." / "The right to psychological continuity can be seen as a special neuro-focused instance of the right to identity." Opening of section 2: "For a long time, the boundaries of the skull have been generally considered the separation line between the observable and unobservable dimension of the living human being."
- **Reliability:** Primary, peer-reviewed, open access. The paper is a normative proposal ("towards"), not a description of existing law; the four rights are the authors' proposals, and only "cognitive liberty" pre-dates them (Sententia, Boire).
- **Relevance to this project:** C20; the neurorights section. The "boundaries of the skull" sentence is a good companion to Bublitz/Richards on the inaccessibility premise.
