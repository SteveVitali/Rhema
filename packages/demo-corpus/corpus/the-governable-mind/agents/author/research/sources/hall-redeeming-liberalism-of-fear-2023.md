# hall-redeeming-liberalism-of-fear-2023
- **URL:** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/189710/1/Shklar_JoP.pdf (accepted-manuscript version; preprint pagination) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, Hall reading of Shklar on private/public intimidation
- **What it establishes:** Hall argues the liberalism of fear is not merely anti-state: it targets intimidation from private and corporate actors too, while public cruelty "must come first." Important caveat: the phrases "intimidation from either public or private agents" and "avoidable fear" are Hall quoting Shklar's 1992 essay "Rights in the Liberal Tradition" (abbreviated RLT; RLT, 30), NOT the 1989 "Liberalism of Fear" essay. The "must come first" sentence is Hall's own gloss.
- **Key passages:**
  - Preprint p. 13 (PDF p. 14): "she insists that the liberalism of fear does not merely aim to eradicate political 'terror' but also to restrain all the sources of 'avoidable fear'. In this sense, the liberalism of fear pushes for a politics that frees all subjects from 'intimidation from either public or private agents'."
  - Preprint p. 13: liberals of fear "'favour the decrease in every form of social inequality' because they recognise that 'Any concentrations of social power that expose people to fears of deprivation of employment, health and education is objectionable'" (Shklar, RLT, 30).
  - Preprint p. 16 (PDF p. 17): "To be sure, Shklar still stresses that the greatest political evil is the sustained infliction of public cruelty. It must come first. But liberals of fear should also concern themselves with combatting intimidation and inequality emanating from private and corporate sub-state actors."
  - Preprint p. 16: this "pushes liberals of fear to attend to nongovernmental sources of fear, such as those generated by market inequality."
  - Abstract: the article "seeks to correct several prominent misreadings of Judith Shklar's liberalism of fear in recent scholarship."
- **Reliability:** Secondary, peer-reviewed (Journal of Politics). Author-produced accepted version under CC BY-NC; page numbers here are preprint pages, not the published 1064–1078 range.
- **Relevance to this project:** Supports the essay's use of Shklar against private/corporate intimidation, but the essay should attribute "public or private agents" to Shklar's "Rights in the Liberal Tradition" (in *The Bill of Rights and the Liberal Tradition*, ed. Timothy Fuller, Colorado Springs: Colorado College Press, 1992, 26–39) via Hall. Reference: Edward Hall, "Complacent and Conservative? Redeeming the Liberalism of Fear," *The Journal of Politics* 85, no. 3 (2023): 1064–1078, https://doi.org/10.1086/722343.
