# openai-helping-people-when-they-need-it-most
- **URL:** https://openai.com/index/helping-people-when-they-need-it-most/ (read via a reader proxy) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, item 4
- **What it establishes:** OpenAI's Aug. 26, 2025 post (date not shown in the extracted text; Slashdot coverage dates it late Aug. 2025). Conversations in which users are "planning to harm others" are routed to "specialized pipelines" reviewed by "a small team trained on our usage policies" who can ban accounts; if reviewers find "an imminent threat of serious physical harm to others," OpenAI "may refer it to law enforcement." Self-harm cases are not referred, "to respect people's privacy given the uniquely private nature of ChatGPT interactions." Also: classifier-based automatic blocking, 988 referrals, 90+ physicians, safeguards degrading in long conversations, planned parental controls and emergency-contact features.
- **Key passages:** "When we detect users who are planning to harm others, we route their conversations to specialized pipelines where they are reviewed by a small team trained on our usage policies and who are authorized to take action, including banning accounts." / "If human reviewers determine that a case involves an imminent threat of serious physical harm to others, we may refer it to law enforcement." / "We are currently not referring self-harm cases to law enforcement to respect people's privacy given the uniquely private nature of ChatGPT interactions."
- **Reliability:** Primary (company statement); interested party.
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 4; the provider itself describes human review and a law-enforcement referral channel, while conceding the "uniquely private nature" of the record.
