# farahany-2023-cognitive-liberty-generative-ai
- **URL:** https://unlocked.microsoft.com/ai-anthology/nita-farahany/ · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, finding (c) "Farahany should be cited"
- **Citation:** Nita A. Farahany, "Cultivating cognitive liberty in the age of generative AI," Microsoft *AI Anthology* (June 19, 2023). Companion: Farahany, "'Cognitive Liberty' Is the Human Right We Need to Talk About," *TIME* (June 26, 2023), https://time.com/6289229/cognitive-liberty-human-right/. Book: Nita A. Farahany, *The Battle for Your Brain: Defending the Right to Think Freely in the Age of Neurotechnology* (New York: St. Martin's Press, 2023).
- **What it establishes:** Farahany's definition of cognitive liberty and its three components; confirms (per the fetched text of both pieces) that she treats generative AI under manipulation/bias/reliability-of-information, and that neither piece treats chat transcripts or AI conversation as extended cognition or as records of thought. The TIME piece does not mention generative AI or chatbots at all; its concern is neurotechnology (EEG/EMG) and corporate/government access to brain data.
- **Key passages:**
  - Microsoft (June 19, 2023): cognitive liberty is "a right to self-determination over our brains and mental experiences, which includes a right to access and use technologies that change our brains if we choose to do so. And it is also a right from unwanted interference with our mental privacy and freedom of thought."
  - Microsoft: AI tools "can exacerbate biases and distortions that undermine the integrity and reliability of the information we consume, in turn influencing our beliefs, judgments, and decisions"; the risk is the potential "to interfere with and manipulate our mental experiences." She describes her own GPT-4 use as an external tool whose outputs she must verify — not as extension of thought.
  - TIME (June 26, 2023): the right to "self-determination over our brains and mental… experiences," which "protect[s] our mental privacy and freedom of thought."
- **Reliability:** Primary for Farahany's own position (popular pieces by the author; the book is the full statement). Not independent of her advocacy for a cognitive-liberty framework.
- **Relevance to this project:** Confirms the gap the essay fills: the dominant cognitive-liberty framing treats AI as a manipulator of the mind, not as a medium in which thinking is done and recorded. Cite her three-part definition, then differentiate.
