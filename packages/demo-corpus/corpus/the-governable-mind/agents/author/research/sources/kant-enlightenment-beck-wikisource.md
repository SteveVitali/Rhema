# kant-enlightenment-beck-wikisource
- **URL:** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/What_is_Enlightenment%3F · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 drafting, claim C9
- **What it establishes:** English text of Kant's "An Answer to the Question: What Is Enlightenment?" (1784) in Lewis White Beck's translation, from *Immanuel Kant, On History* (Indianapolis: Bobbs-Merrill, 1963), as the page identifies it. (The Columbia University copy of the same translation, the outline's likely lead, sits behind a Cloudflare challenge for automated access.) Beck renders Unmündigkeit as "tutelage."
- **Key passages:**
  - "Enlightenment is man's release from his self-incurred tutelage. Tutelage is man's inability to make use of his understanding without direction from another."
  - "_Sapere aude!_ 'Have courage to use your own reason!' - that is the motto of enlightenment."
  - "After the guardians have first made their domestic cattle dumb and have made sure that these placid creatures will not dare take a single step without the harness of the cart to which they are tethered, the guardians then show them the danger which threatens if they try to go alone."
  - "By the public use of one's reason I understand the use which a person makes of it as a scholar before the reading public. Private use I call that which one may make of it in a particular civil post or office which is entrusted to him."
- **Reliability:** Primary text in a named 20th-century translation (Beck, 1963; still in copyright, so quote with attribution). Beck's "tutelage" is the older rendering; Gregor's "minority" and Nisbet's "immaturity" are the Cambridge renderings.
- **Relevance to this project:** C9 — confirms public use = the scholar's use before the reading public; private use = use in a civil post.
