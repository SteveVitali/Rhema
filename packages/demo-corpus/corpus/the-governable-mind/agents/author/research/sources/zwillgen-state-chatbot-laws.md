# zwillgen-state-chatbot-laws
- **URL:** https://www.zwillgen.com/publication/chatbot-laws-coming-to-a-state-near-you/ (reprint of IAPP article by Andrew Eichen and Brenda Leong) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 response round, item 12
- **What it establishes:** As of June 2026, 11 states have passed chatbot laws (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Georgia, Idaho, Iowa, Nebraska, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Washington), with Hawaii awaiting signature (12 counted). Common structure: AI-identity disclosure; self-harm protocols; minor protections. Every law "requires the operator to maintain a protocol for detecting expressions of suicidal ideation or self-harm and referring such users to crisis resources"; five require "evidence-based methods" for detection. Reporting: several require publishing protocols and annual crisis-referral counts (Colorado, Georgia, Oregon, Washington on the website; California, Colorado, Hawaii, Rhode Island annual state reports). Age: Colorado requires "commercially reasonable or generally accepted methods to estimate or obtain the age of all users"; Georgia requires age assurance before sexual-content features; New York's pending expansion bill requires age assurance for companion-style features with "at least one method [that] must not require a government ID or must let the user remain anonymous to the operator," self-attestation insufficient. Nothing in the survey requires conversation logging/retention or identity verification of general users; Colorado gives minors control over retention and training use. Effective dates mostly Jan./July 2027; NY in force Nov. 5, 2025.
- **Key passages:** "Each law requires the operator to maintain a protocol for detecting expressions of suicidal ideation or self-harm and referring such users to crisis resources, such as a suicide hotline, crisis text line, or the 988 Lifeline." / "Colorado requires operators of conversational AI systems to use commercially reasonable or generally accepted methods to estimate or obtain the age of all users." / NY bill: "at least one method must not require a government ID or must let the user remain anonymous to the operator and that a user's self-attestation is not sufficient". / "Colorado, Georgia, Oregon and Washington require operators to include the number of crisis referrals made in the previous year."
- **Reliability:** Secondary (law-firm survey, IAPP-published); undated on the page but states "As of June 2026." Independent of operators.
- **Relevance to this project:** Item 12; establishes that the state-law layer mandates detection/referral (a form of institutional legibility) without mandating retention or identity—the absence the essay can point to. Note "none requires logging/retention" is an inference from silence in this survey, not an explicit statement in it.
