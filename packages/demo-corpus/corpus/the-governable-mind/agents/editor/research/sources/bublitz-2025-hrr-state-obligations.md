# bublitz-2025-hrr-state-obligations
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12142-025-00751-0 (reached via Springer cookie-fallback; article page read) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 legal/philosophical
- **What it establishes:** Bublitz, "Freedom of Thought and Digital Technologies: Operationalizing State Obligations for Social Media, Virtual Reality, and Neurotechnology," Human Rights Review 26:4 (2025). Structures obligations as respect/protect/fulfil: states must not "(i) alter a person's thought without consent; (ii) forcibly reveal a person's unexpressed thought without consent"; must take "appropriate legislative, administrative, budgetary, judicial and other measures," proposing "freedom-of-thought impact assessment" and "freedom-of-thought-sensitive technology design." Thoughts may be revealed by "inferences from biometric signals or digital surveillance." No treatment of chatbots, chat logs, or retained records.
- **Key passages:** "Freedom of thought is among the strongest human rights, yet it is largely devoid of practical legal application." · "inferences from biometric signals or digital surveillance."
- **Reliability:** Primary; peer-reviewed.
- **Relevance to this project:** Ref [22], l.66. Confirms the dormancy quotation and supplies the respect/protect/fulfil structure the draft could use to name the state's negative duty over the record; the record itself is absent from his examples.
