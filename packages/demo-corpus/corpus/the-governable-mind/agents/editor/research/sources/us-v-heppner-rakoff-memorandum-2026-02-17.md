# us-v-heppner-rakoff-memorandum-2026-02-17
- **URL:** https://www.akingump.com/a/web/ssTGsd5NHbtZ1onzXQMTye/1_25-cr-503-27-memorandum.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 1 slice A (via a reader proxy; law-firm mirror of Dkt. 27)
- **What it establishes:** United States v. Heppner, No. 25-cr-00503 (JSR), Dkt. 27 (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 17, 2026), memorandum following a Feb. 10 bench ruling. Three independent grounds against privilege (not an attorney; no confidentiality; not for legal advice); ~31 Claude documents seized from devices during a Nov. 4, 2025 search of Heppner's home; Anthropic's policy on retention, training, and disclosure defeats confidentiality; work product rejected. Reaches the primary the author had only through the HLR Blog (III19, V3).
- **Key passages:** pp. 5–6: "Because Claude is not an attorney, see ECF No. 23-6, that alone disposes of Heppner's claim of privilege." · p. 3: agents "executed a search warrant at Heppner's home and seized numerous documents and electronic devices"; "approximately thirty-one documents that memorialize communications that Heppner had with the generative AI platform 'Claude.'" · pp. 6–7: Anthropic "collects data on both users' 'inputs' and Claude's 'outputs,' that it uses such data to 'train' Claude, and that Anthropic reserves the right to disclose such data to a host of 'third parties,' including 'governmental regulatory authorities'" · p. 7: "Heppner could have had no 'reasonable expectation of confidentiality in his communications' with Claude."
