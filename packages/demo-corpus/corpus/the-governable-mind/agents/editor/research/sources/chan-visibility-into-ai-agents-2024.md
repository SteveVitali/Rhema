# chan-visibility-into-ai-agents-2024
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13138v4 · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 policy
- **What it establishes:** Chan et al., 'Visibility into AI Agents,' ACM FAccT 2024 (full HTML read). Three measures: agent identifiers, real-time monitoring, activity logs. Logs defined to include user inputs; linking actions to user/developer/deployer is a stated benefit; user authentication contemplated; surveillance abuse by governments/deployers acknowledged and mitigated by minimum-necessary access, not by object separation; monitoring users of a few compute providers ≈ monitoring society.
- **Key passages:** 'Activity logs are records of certain inputs and outputs of an agent. Inputs and outputs could be from both users or tools and services' (3.3) · 'allow one to tie behaviour to specific user choices, facilitating post-incident attribution' (3.3) · 'accountability by linking an action to an AI agent and its user, developer(s), and deployer' (3.1.1) · 'Authentication of the agent's user may be useful when individuals are providing sensitive information' (3.1.2) · 'Governments or deployers may thus abuse their power to carry out excessive or unjustified surveillance of personal activities' (3.4) · 'The granularity and quantity of access should be the minimum necessary' (3.4).
- **Reliability:** Primary; GovAI-affiliated authors; safety-aligned.
- **Relevance to this project:** Q3: the direct predecessor the draft's l.234/l.244 distinction must cite against; it lacks the distinction and proposes the identity linkage.
