# lu-compute-monitoring-jolt-2025
- **URL:** https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v39.1/Compute-Monitoring.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 review, Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Lisa Lu, Harvard JOLT 39:1 (Fall 2025; full PDF extracted): compute monitoring revives the encryption-backdoor debate; compelled access to encrypted/confidential-computing data may be an unconstitutional search; mandated backdoors are compelled speech; permissible objective metrics vs invasive disclosures; third-party doctrine protects content less than metrics.
- **Key passages:** "government-compelled access to user data through compute providers…may constitute an unconstitutional search." · "requiring providers to build decryption mechanisms or backdoors constitutes compelled speech." · "the Fourth Amendment likely does not protect non-content data, such as compute metrics."
- **Reliability:** Primary; student note, Stanford; independent.
- **Relevance to this project:** Clipper parallel (l.142–146); Q3 metrics/content line; §4(c).
