# bublitz-2026-what-is-thought-ch8-springer-page
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-91466-9_8 (reached via Springer cookie-fallback; open-access chapter page read, PDF not retrievable today) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 legal/philosophical
- **What it establishes:** Bublitz, "What Is 'Thought'? Interpreting and Constructing Article 18 ICCPR in Light of the Vienna Convention," in Bublitz & Blitz (eds.), Law and Ethics of Freedom of Thought vol. 2 (2026), pp. 217-254. Proposes a "Robust-scope view": thought is "thoughts as a type of occurrent mental state with content, and thinking as a mental action," including calculating and imagining, possibly excluding "mental states without intentional objects such as moods." Does not treat notes, diaries, data, devices, or machine conversations as thought; manifestations fall under Article 19.
- **Key passages:** "Many ways to manifest thought fall under the ambit of the subsequent Article 19 ICCPR, freedom of expression." · "generalizations from the externum to the internum, and vice versa, are not readily possible."
- **Reliability:** Primary; the leading scope theorist; the chapter is the current statement of the narrow view (2013 fn. 23 updated).
- **Relevance to this project:** Agenda 4; the strongest published counterargument to l.76 ("descends from the interior" is a generalization from internum to externum, which Bublitz says is "not readily possible"). Ligthart's reply chapter (pp. 255-266) is the in-volume rejoinder and was not readable today.
