# ca-sb1119-assembly-privacy-analysis-2026-07-01
- **URL:** https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/media/1209 (PDF, 26 pp., text-extracted) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 (agenda 3)
- **What it establishes:** Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection analysis of SB 1119 (Padilla), "Companion chatbots: children's safety," as amended June 25, 2026; hearing July 1, 2026; Senate vote 39-0. Builds on SB 243 (Ch. 677, Stats. 2025) after Newsom's veto of AB 1064 and after Common Sense Media and OpenAI "teamed up ... to introduce a ballot measure" then "halted the effort." The bill "would require developers to: Implement age assurance. Operators would have to comply with AB 1043 (Wicks, Ch. 675, Stats. 2025), which establishes an age verification system for users of mobile devices and computers. Beginning in 2027, parents who allow their children to be the main users of such devices will be able to configure the device to send a non-identifying age bracket signal – under 13, between 13 and 16, between 16 and 18, or at least 18 – that operating systems and application sto[res pass on]." Other duties by July 1, 2027: documented child-safety risk assessment; crisis protocol with parent notification within 12 hours where accounts are linked; default "ephemeral mode" for children ("any conversational history, interaction log, or user-provided personal input is permanently deleted from the operator's systems within 48 hours") unless a parent consents to "persistent conversational memory"; time limits; parental controls; bans on "excessively sycophantic" responses; audits. Uses SB 243's definition of companion chatbot, which the analysis and industry opponents say "scopes in general-purpose AI systems" ("General purpose models such as ChatGPT and Gemini can also be companion chatbots"). Oakland Privacy recommends "applying the bill to all users, not just children." Opposition: CalChamber coalition, TechNet, American Innovators Network.
- **Key passages:** "Implement age assurance ... a non-identifying age bracket signal"; "ephemeral mode"; "persistent conversational memory"; "scopes in general-purpose AI systems."
- **Reliability:** Primary legislative analysis (committee staff); neutral.
- **Relevance to this project:** Agenda 3. The age-assurance mechanism is a device-level, non-identifying age-bracket signal (AB 1043), not identity verification; but the bill also legislates the retention architecture of layer 3 for minors (ephemeral by default, memory by parental consent), which is a direct instance of the essay's third question at l.244.
