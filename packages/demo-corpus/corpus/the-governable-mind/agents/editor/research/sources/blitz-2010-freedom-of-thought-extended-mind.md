# blitz-2010-freedom-of-thought-extended-mind
- **URL:** https://scholarship.law.okcu.edu/downloads/d7kcg-2ck33/fulltext.pdf · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v1 review, Phase 2
- **What it establishes:** Blitz, 2010 Wis. L. Rev. 1049 (read in full). Freedom of thought protects 'activity that is the functional equivalent of thought' including journals and iPhones, built on Clark & Chalmers and the diary; concern is state prohibition of cognitive tools, not surveillance.
- **Key passages:** "activity that is either (1) the functional equivalent of thought, or (2) a reshaping of the self that does that thinking" · "If Otto receives First Amendment protection when he inscribes the information he perceives into a journal, why not ... a brain chip" · "someone's regular and automatic use of a journal or iPhone to recall information"
- **Reliability:** Primary scholarly source; independent.
- **Relevance to this project:** Report §1.1, §2, §3: the direct predecessor of the 'externalized forum internum' move (draft l.82–86).
