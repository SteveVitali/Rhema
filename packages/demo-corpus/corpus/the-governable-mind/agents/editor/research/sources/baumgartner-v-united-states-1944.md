# baumgartner-v-united-states-1944
- **URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/322/665 · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 1 slice B
- **What it establishes:** Baumgartner v. United States, 322 U.S. 665 (1944), was a denaturalization suit (certificate of 1932) in which the Government relied mainly on the defendant's private diary (Dec. 1, 1938 – summer 1941) and seven years of pro-Nazi statements; the Court held that post-naturalization expressions cannot by presumption prove a fraudulent oath and reversed. Confirms the draft's "a private diary offered as evidence against its author" and Blitz's fn. 103 characterization.
- **Key passages:** "the diary which Baumgartner kept from December 1, 1938, to the summer of 1941 reveals that he attended a meeting of the German Vocational League … Other diary entries reflect violent anti-semitism" · "Baumgartner's diary, on which the Government mainly relies, reveals that when in 1939 he attended a meeting of the German Vocational League … it was apparently his only experience with this group" · proof of state of mind at the oath "can be removed, if at all, only by a presumption that disqualifying views expressed after naturalization were accurate representations" (rejected).
- **Reliability:** Primary (U.S. Reports text via Cornell LII).
- **Relevance to this project:** III9 (l.74); the diary-in-miniature point is stronger than the draft states.
