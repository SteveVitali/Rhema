# bedi-2021-myth-chilling-effect-fulltext
- **URL:** https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v35/Bedi-The-Myth-of-the-Chilling-Effect.pdf (PDF downloaded and text-extracted; read in full for design and conclusions) · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 (agenda 4)
- **What it establishes:** Suneal Bedi, "The Myth of the Chilling Effect," 35 Harv. J.L. & Tech. 267 (2021). Experimental study of a private social-media speech restriction (online restaurant reviews), varying how broad the restriction is, with LIWC text analysis and third-party raters. Conclusion: "in the context of social media, the chilling effect has little to no impact on the content of the message; at most, it slightly alters the specific style or tone used in speech. The study showed that speech did change in the face of social media censorship - it become more positive and civil. However, the overall communicative effect of the speech did not change - the reviews were just as negative as reviews with no censorship." Frames the doctrine as resting, in Schauer's words, on "unpersuasive empirical guesswork." The object is public posting under a content rule (a speech restriction), not private inquiry under observation; the article does not study search, reading, or questioning behaviour.
- **Key passages:** as quoted; Part IV argues chilling may "promote speech rather than frustrate it" by producing "more civil speech."
- **Reliability:** Primary scholarly; single experiment; independent.
- **Relevance to this project:** Agenda 4 (l.86). Bedi cuts against the general chilling thesis but not against l.86's mechanism: his manipulation is a rule about what may be posted, and his outcome is the content of posts; the essay's mechanism is uncertainty about who may later read what one asked, and its outcome is what one asks. Penney 2022's conformity model (already cited) is the better-matched account, and Bedi's "more civil" finding is, for a medium of private inquiry, itself the injury (the draft's "ask them less honestly," l.90).
