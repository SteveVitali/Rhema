# mccarthy-jones-2019-autonomous-mind-frontiers
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frai.2019.00019/full · **Accessed:** 2026-09-16 · **For:** v2 review, Phase 2 legal/philosophical
- **What it establishes:** Simon McCarthy-Jones, "The Autonomous Mind: The Right to Freedom of Thought in the Twenty-First Century," Frontiers in AI (2019). Three elements of the right (not to reveal; not to be punished; not to be manipulated). Applies Clark & Chalmers to argue that external activities can be thought and that "Internet searches have become thought" for people who consult search engines habitually and trust the results; the legal stake is that what is classed as "thought" gets the right's absolute protection while what is classed as "privacy" gets qualified protection.
- **Key passages:** "the use of pen-and-paper to perform calculations or the re-arrangement of Scrabble tiles to make words, constitutes thinking." · "Internet searches have become thought." · "The right to FoT should protect thought wherever it is found, not just in the head."
- **Reliability:** Primary; peer-reviewed; the author is a psychologist and advocate of a broad reading.
- **Relevance to this project:** Agenda 4 (l.74-76). A published position stronger than the draft's: the record's protection does not merely descend from the interior, the record is thought and inherits absoluteness. The draft sits between this and De Cock and should say so.
