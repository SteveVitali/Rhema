- **What it establishes:** Keese & Leiser, ch. 25 (pp. 321-334) of O'Callaghan & Shiner (eds.), The Cambridge Handbook of the Right to Freedom of Thought (CUP, March 6, 2025). Thesis: "manipulative online practices interfere with freedom of thought when they affect one's mental autonomy in a sufficiently global manner," with factors of concealment, vulnerability and power asymmetry, the importance of the targeted thoughts, and frequency/duration. Part VI neighbours: ch. 24 "What Is Thought and What Makes It Free?" (McCarthy-Jones & Walmsley); ch. 26 "Neurorights"; ch. 27 "Freedom of Thought" [in criminal justice] (Ligthart & Van De Pol); ch. 28 "Realising the Societal Dimensions of the Right to Freedom of Thought in the Digital Age through Strategic Litigation"; ch. 29 "Non-ideal Theory and Protecting Freedom of Thought." The chapter is about manipulation (dark patterns, targeting), not about records, retention, or state access.
- **Key passages:** "sufficiently global manner."
- **Reliability:** Primary abstract (publisher page); full text paywalled.
- **Relevance to this project:** Agenda 4; ref [22]. Confirms that the Handbook's digital chapter is manipulation-centred, like Alegre and Farahany; the observation-and-permission axis is the draft's.
