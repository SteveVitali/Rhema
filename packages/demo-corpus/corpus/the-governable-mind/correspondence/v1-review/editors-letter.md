## Verdict

**Major revisions.** This is a serious, unusually well-sourced political-philosophical essay with three contributions that would survive any amount of cutting: the Stationers' Company as the shared origin of press freedom and public-private information control; the five layers ordered by what governing each must know about a person; and the observation that a synthesis is not a record. Its factual substrate is sound in a way rare in the genre: of 79 claims checked to primary sources, none is contradicted. What stands between it and publication is not research or prose but three things the draft declines to say and one thing it does not know. It never runs its own test on the one policy question the whole Milton apparatus was built to answer (pre-release certification of open weights). It enters the freedom-of-thought field without knowing that field's founding article makes its central move, or that the field has since abandoned the absolutism the draft opens with. It calls itself "anticipatory" on a subject where the record has already caught up: chat logs are warranted and adjudicated objects, and in June 2026 a liberal state made identity a condition of capable inference for eighteen days by an instrument the essay's test never examines. And the constructive half (§§XI–XV) is starved by a history that takes 62 percent of the length. None of this requires new argument; all of it requires the author to write down conclusions the draft already implies.

**The one thing:** at l.266, run the seven questions on mandatory pre-release testing for open-weight release at a capability threshold, which is what Anthropic proposes, what the White House has said it will extend the Executive Order's framework to, and what, in the essay's own §VI terms, is certification before encounter administered through a small number of private bodies with the state's blessing. Say which questions it passes, which it fails, and what a version that passed would need. The essay exists to write that paragraph, and the second reader and I reached that conclusion independently.

## The piece's project

The essay argues that liberalism's boundary between inward judgment and outward conduct rested on an unstated fact: judgment was unreachable, so its protection cost nothing and needed no doctrine. Artificial intelligence moves a large part of deliberation into technical systems where it becomes recordable, classifiable by machine, and gate-able by permission. Governing the machines therefore threatens to become governing the thinking done with them, and the constitutional problem of AI governance is to govern dangerous artificial capability without making lawful human cognition legible or permissioned. The instruments are a layered model of what "AI regulation" touches, a seven-question diagnostic for any proposed architecture, a named failure mode (the public-private cognitive-security complex) with four necessary conditions, and the insistence that permissionless epistemic exit be preserved whatever the market decides. A subordinate claim runs through it: the July 2026 OpenAI/Hugging Face incident is evidence for the safety case and the exit case at once, and an honest argument holds both.

The skeleton is a reconstruction of the toleration tradition (§I), the cognitive-science case that the mind was never wholly inside the skull, taken at its weakest defensible strength (§II), the legal right waiting to be applied and the new object it must apply to (§III), three liberties converging on one object (§IV), the state/corporation asymmetry and the definition of exit (§V), the chokepoint genealogy from the Stationers to Clipper (§VI), cognitive legibility as the successor to the previous essay's behavioral legibility (§VII), the authoritarian demonstrations (§VIII), probability shaping and the synthesis/record problem (§IX), the hard case (§X), the layers (§XI), the test (§XII), the complex (§XIII), permissioned cognition and what would falsify the warning (§XIV), and pluralism (§XV). The independent second reader, given only the draft, stated the thesis in terms materially identical to mine, so the piece transmits its own thesis.

Success on its own terms would be a technically and philosophically literate reader who came in thinking the debate was "safety versus openness" and leaves with a different first question (what is being controlled), a vocabulary exact enough to say which regulations touch deliberation and which do not, and a reason to treat open and local models as constitutionally significant regardless of market share.

## Strengths

Protect these through revision.

- **The prologue's two halves and the refusal to let either camp own them** (l.10–16). The ledger verifies the incident to OpenAI's and Hugging Face's own posts (C1–C9); the trade press reached the same "paradox" framing, but the symmetry, the disclosure of Hugging Face's interest, and the pivot to the prior question are the essay's. Better supported than the draft knows: the Hugging Face timeline names Anthropic's models verbatim, and SaferAI's independent evaluation found GLM-5.2 refused none of the offensive cyber or biology tasks it was given while Claude refused so consistently the benchmark could not be completed (policy report, Headline 5).
- **"This essay does not need the third proposition"** (l.58–62). Declining the strong extended-mind thesis is the respected move in the philosophy-of-mind literature (Blitz uses "functional equivalent"; Tesink et al. adopt the thesis only for argument's sake), and it is what protects the essay from the over-breadth objection. The argument is right even where the paragraphs echo (see Line notes).
- **"The developmental history of a belief"** (l.88). Sharper than "profiling" or "search history," and already instantiated in a charging document: the Palisades-fire indictment used a hypothetical ChatGPT question as evidence of state of mind (legal report §6).
- **Exit measured against market share** (l.120–124). "Market share measures what people choose. Exit measures what they could choose if the choosing were made to cost them." No prior statement of the constitutionally-significant-while-economically-marginal argument was found in either discourse stratum.
- **The Stationers' genealogy** (l.128–138, l.256). On every search run, original to the AI-regulation discourse. The two cautions at l.132 make the mechanism more credible, not less. "The Stationers were a chokepoint before they were a censor" is the sentence the piece should be known for.
- **The Stasi paragraph** (l.174–180). "Artificial intelligence cheapens interpretation. That is what it is for." The best line in §VIII and the point the whole section exists to make.
- **A synthesis is not a record** (l.202). The most original idea in the essay, located exactly in Arendt's premise. The second reader independently named it the strongest thing in the piece.
- **The five layers ordered by what governing each must know** (l.226–244). The lifecycle split is standard; the ordering criterion is the essay's, and it is what makes the layers do civil-libertarian rather than engineering work. Question 3, "whether compliance can be established while revealing less than the rule's drafters first asked for" (l.254), is a diagnostic a policy reader could adopt tomorrow.
- **The four-condition definition with its list of non-instances** (l.272–278). No counterpart found. It is the essay's best defense against the "everything is the complex" reading; keep l.276 exactly as it is.
- **The definition of permissioned cognition** (l.292) and **the falsification paragraph** (l.294). The first is the best paragraph in the piece. The second is a move almost no essay of this kind makes, and it should survive every revision, corrected as Major issue 2 requires.
- **The essay argues against its own side** (l.268): a local model given a network interface is layer 5 and "the freedom of thought does not follow it there." Keep this; it is what earns the essay the right to be believed on layer 4.

## Major issues

### 1. The test is never run on the case it was built for: model distribution

- **Where:** l.230 ("Model distribution is the second: the release or transfer of trained weights. This is the hard case"); l.234 (exit "lives" at layer 4); l.218 (Anthropic: "all sufficiently capable models, open and closed, should go through mandatory safety testing," quoted approvingly as evidence against the capture story); l.266–268 (the test run only on the Order's classified benchmarking and on the essay's own local-inference preference at layer 5); l.294 ("unsigned weights a legal hazard" as a falsification condition); l.38 and l.136 (*Areopagitica* as the reply to certification before encounter).
- **The problem:** Whether there is anything to run at layer 4 is decided at layer 2. The essay names layer 2 the hard case, quotes the proposal actually on the table (Anthropic's mandatory pre-release testing "before release"; the White House's stated plan to extend the Order's pre-release framework to open-weight models at frontier capability, WIRED via AI Weekly, Aug. 17, policy report §4(a)), loads the Milton apparatus in §I and §VI, and then tests only the easy case and its own side. A capability-conditioned rule at layer 2 caps permissionless exit below the frontier by construction, and the essay's exit exhibit was valuable in the prologue precisely because GLM-5.2 was near-frontier and unrestricted. The essay's own falsification clause names the layer-2 outcome ("unsigned weights a legal hazard") as the sign it was "early," without noticing that the proposal it just credited produces it. The seven-question test has an uncited predecessor here too: Sastry et al. (2024) state that compute governance "can be used to infringe on civil liberties, prop up the powerful, and entrench authoritarian regimes" and propose five guardrails that overlap questions 1, 2/5, 3, 6 and 7 (policy report §4(c)). Without a citation the test reads as reinvention; with one, the essay's additions (chokepoint creation, exit, repurposability, and deliberation rather than data as the object) are visible as additions.
- **Why it matters here:** A senior reader concludes the author knows the answer and does not want to print it. The safety community will say the essay's exit is either sub-frontier (then why the prologue?) or a demand for irreversible proliferation dressed as conscience; the open-weights community will say the essay concedes mandatory testing without objection at l.218. Both are hostile-but-fair readings of a silence.
- **Direction:** One paragraph at l.266. My reading of the essay's own premises: capability-conditioned testing at layer 2 passes question 3 (it needs to know about models, not persons) and question 5 in the near term (sub-threshold weights stay free), strains question 4 (a small number of testers become a gateway for the trade, the Stationers' shape) and question 7 (thresholds ratchet; a body that certifies release does not dissolve), and is acceptable only if the certifying body's classifications are inspectable and appealable (question 6), the threshold is not defined by what users ask, and enforcement never reaches layer 4 (no signature requirement at the point of execution, l.286). Say that, or say the opposite, but say it. Cite Sastry at l.238 or l.248 and state in one sentence what the seven questions add. Show the test passing a real safety bill too: the Lieu–Moran AI Kill Switch Act (July 23) is "remotely revocable capability" (l.146) scoped to developers at layers 1 and 5, and a pass on it is the essay's best answer to the "shield for one side" charge (policy report §6).

### 2. The essay tests the text of June 2026 and misses its practice; "anticipatory" is behind the record

- **Where:** l.20 ("As of this writing no liberal state has built the architecture the later sections describe"; "The argument is anticipatory rather than accusatory"); l.218 (the Order "provides that 'nothing in this section shall be construed to authorize the creation of a mandatory governmental licensing, preclearance, or permitting requirement'"); l.266 ("on this test it is close to harmless"; "A voluntary framework passes the reversibility question for now"); l.286 ("Powerful inference requires an identity, because accountability requires a name"); l.294 ("If instead an identity has become a condition of capable inference … it will have been early"); l.88–92 and l.276 ("A provider that answers a lawful subpoena does not constitute it").
- **The problem, part one (the architecture):** Ten days after the Order was signed, on June 12, 2026, the Commerce Department directed Anthropic by an unpublished BIS "is informed" letter under the Export Administration Regulations to bar every foreign national worldwide from its two most capable models. Unable to verify nationality in real time, Anthropic shut both models off for everyone for eighteen days, then introduced biometric identity verification through Persona for consumer tiers from July 8; its most capable current model is available only through identity-verified trusted-access programs, and OpenAI's counterpart requires government ID. In the same weeks the administration used the Order's "voluntary" channel to hold OpenAI's GPT-5.6 to about twenty approved partners for roughly two weeks; OpenAI complied while saying this "should not become the long-term default." Sources and precisions in the policy report, Headline 1 and §4(d); ledger C15 re-scored Misleading in context on this evidence. So: identity became a condition of capable hosted inference in a liberal state, by a security instrument rather than by "AI safety" regulation (which is the essay's own §VIII thesis at l.182 landing at home), and it left verification infrastructure behind when the directive was lifted (the essay's own question 7 in miniature). The verdict at l.266 is true of the Order's text and false of the practice beside it, and the falsification clause at l.294 is presented as untriggered when its first condition has fired.
- **The problem, part two (the record):** The cognitive record is not a forecast. Between May 2025 and June 2026 it became a preserved, sampled, warranted, and adjudicated object: the *NYT v. OpenAI* preservation order (May–Oct. 2025) made every consumer's deletion inoperative for four months; a Maine warrant (Sept. 2025) identified a suspect *by the content of his prompts*, which is cognitive legibility's first documented instance and should be its example at l.154; OpenAI's Aug. 2025 policy routes conversations to human review with a standing channel to law enforcement; a federal judge ordered 20 million logs produced (Jan. 2026) on the reasoning that users "voluntarily submitted their communications"; *United States v. Heppner* (Feb. 2026) held a defendant's Claude prompts unprivileged, turning partly on the provider's retention terms, so a locally run model would have produced no third-party record at all, which is the best illustration of epistemic exit available anywhere and the essay does not use it; *United States v. Kim* (June 2026) obtained a defendant's whole deliberative record from OpenAI and Anthropic under ordinary stored-communications process; content warrants roughly tripled between OpenAI's two transparency reports. Full table with sources: legal report §6.
- **Why it matters here:** A draft that says "anticipatory" in September 2026 to readers who have followed *NYT v. OpenAI*, the Maine warrant, *Heppner*, *Kim*, and the June directive reads as unread on its own subject, and the philosophy will not save it with that audience. The policy press's strongest hostile-but-fair reading is simply that the factual predicate at l.20 is false as of June 2026.
- **Direction:** Split the framing rather than drop it. The *record* is present tense: put the table's four strongest items into §III at l.88–92 and into §XI at l.232, and add at l.276 the companion sentence that twenty million conversations were produced under lawful process in January 2026, so "lawful subpoena" is no longer a limiting case. The *composed architecture* is still prospective: keep l.20 for that, but say so. At l.218 add the June record and the labs' identity-tier answer to the second revelation (the labs answered "guardrails refused the defenders" with permission, not exit; that converts §XIV from a construction into a partial description). At l.266 test the practice as well as the text. At l.294 make the June directive the first datum in the essay's own falsification test; that is the honest and the more powerful move. Cite OpenAI's own phrase for the record, "the uniquely private nature of ChatGPT interactions," against the "users treat chatbots as public" objection.

### 3. §III enters a field it does not know it is in

- **Where:** l.74 (GC22: "does not permit any limitations whatsoever"; "protected unconditionally"); l.76 (Bublitz on dormancy); l.82–86 (the coinage; "the political importance of the diary never depended on ontological identity"; "protection … has always attached to media"); l.254 and l.276 (the essay's own proposed limitations); the subtitle's "Cognitive Liberty"; the absence anywhere of *Smith*, *Miller*, *Carpenter*, *Chatrie*, or "third-party doctrine."
- **The problem:** Four findings from the legal report converge here. (a) The externalized forum internum is an unwitting reinvention of Marc Blitz, "Freedom of Thought for the Extended Mind," 2010 Wis. L. Rev. 1049, which argues from the diary and Otto's notebook that freedom of thought covers "the functional equivalent of thought," including "someone's regular and automatic use of a journal or iPhone." The draft's extension is real and should be claimed: Blitz protects the liberty to *use* the tool against prohibition; the draft protects using it *unobserved and unpermissioned*, and it names a sharper object (the developmental record). But Blitz co-edits the Palgrave volume the draft already cites at [17], and a neurorights reader will assume the author read the footnote and stayed silent. (b) Bublitz, whom the draft cites for dormancy, rejects Blitz's move by name (2013, fn. 23: extending the right to devices "eliminates the distinction between personality and property rights"), and Ligthart (HRLR 2025) argues that "categorical insistence on the absolute nature" of the right "is unpersuasive" and proposes implied limitations for imminent harm and emergency disclosure. The draft opens §III with the absolutism and then proposes limitations itself (incident records at l.254; lawful subpoenas at l.276) without noticing. The published objection lands on the rhetoric and glances off the substance. (c) Alegre (*Freedom to Think*, 2022) is the best-known statement that the absolute right reaches digital inference; Farahany (2023) is the reason "cognitive liberty" is a current term; Sententia and Boire coined it for a different object. None is cited. (d) The diary analogy hides the disanalogy that matters: the diary was in the writer's desk; the cognitive record is in the provider's datacenter, and US law has for fifty years treated that difference as dispositive. The prior essay cited *Carpenter* and *Chatrie*; this one, on a more legally live subject, cites neither, and *Chatrie* (June 29, 2026) narrowed the doctrine on *Carpenter*'s reasoning in the very term the draft was written. The second reader, from the text alone, found the same hole: "a prompt to a hosted service is a communication to a corporation," and the essay proposes no privilege-like doctrine for the provider the way letters to lawyers, confessions, and library records each had to earn one.
- **Why it matters here:** Neurorights scholars will recognize the subject as theirs and the draft as an outsider's who re-derived their founding article; privacy-law scholars will ask about the third-party doctrine in the first minute. The rubric caps originality at 2 for an unwitting reinvention, and this is the finding that does it. The fix lifts the score two points in a paragraph.
- **Direction:** Cite Blitz at l.82 and claim the extension in one sentence. Relocate the right's absoluteness: say GC22's "no limitations whatsoever" belongs to the unmanifested interior, that the externalized record is a new object to which the right's *rationale* (not its unlimited scope) transfers, and that the seven questions of §XII are the essay's proposal for the limitations Ligthart says must be drawn. That turns the field's strongest objection into the essay's own framework. Two or three sentences after l.86 on the third-party doctrine: the law is already deciding, warrant by warrant, whether the medium of deliberation is a bank record or a home, and *Carpenter* and *Chatrie* show it bending toward "indispensable and revealing" media, which is the draft's own description of the object. Then claim the advantage the privacy literature lacks: a warrant requirement governs access; it does not touch retention, classification, or referral, which is where the four conditions of l.272 live. One sentence each for Alegre (whom the draft inverts: she argues from absoluteness to regulating platforms; the draft argues from the same right to a limit on state architecture), Farahany (the neurotech exponent; the draft's case is the one her framework leaves out), and the coinage's origin.

### 4. Exit is under-theorized at four joints

- **Where:** l.120 (definition: "leave one cognitive intermediary for another"); l.122–124 (the permissionless form; the cellar press); l.14 ("the exit, when it was needed, came from Beijing"); l.196–198 (probability shaping as a property of the intermediary); §VIII (China as the demonstration); l.116–118 (Shklar; "private power can never violate freedom" rejected); l.300 (Pettit against the benevolent gatekeeper).
- **The problem:** (a) Exit from a provider is not exit from a trainer. The shaping §IX describes (what is retrieved first, which objections are volunteered, what "quietly ceases to be retrievable") is a property of the weights, and weights carry their trainer's shaping into the cellar; Pettit's uncontestable gatekeeper describes a frozen local model as well as a hosted one. The only exit in the prologue was trained by a Beijing company; the essay's cautionary archive is China; no sentence connects them. The second reader found the same dropped thread. (b) Exit to a weaker model. The cellar press assumes it prints the same books; if frontier capability stays at datacenter scale, exit is structurally second-class, and the essay half-concedes this ("does not require that they win the market," l.124) without saying what it concedes. (c) The state only closes exits. §V grants that private power dominates, but the constructive apparatus never asks the state to *guarantee* exit (portability, interoperability, public compute, published-weights safe harbors), though the republican tradition the essay invokes is precisely the one that assigns the state that role, and OpenAI's own September post makes the republican reply in the incumbent's voice ("democratically accountable standards … would replace that fragmented system of private governance," policy report §4(b)). (d) The Shklar contrast leans harder than her text allows: she counts "corporate business enterprises" as "basically public organizations" that "owe their entire character and power to the laws" (ledger C69, her p. 31), which strengthens the essay's concession at l.118 and weakens the state/corporation opening of §V. Also: Hirschman is never named, and the labs' actual answer to the second revelation was permission (identity-verified tiers), not exit, which the essay should know when it says "the intelligence they controlled themselves would" serve.
- **Why it matters here:** Open-weights readers will read the essay as a brief for Chinese models and safety readers will read "exit came from Beijing" as the essay's confession; both will weaponize l.14. And a reader who accepts non-domination will ask why the remedy is not accountable public standards, which is the objection the essay half-answers.
- **Direction:** A paragraph at the end of §V or in §XV: exit is plural or it is nothing; plurality among *trainers* is the requirement, and the origin of a model is a sovereignty problem, not a liberty problem, unless a single trainer becomes the only exit. Name Hirschman once. State that democratically accountable standards at layers 1 and 5 are the republican remedy and pass the test, and that the objection bites only when "accountable standards" are enforced at layer 3 by making the user legible. Acknowledge Shklar's corporate sentence; it helps. Say in one sentence what exit to a weaker model is worth (the licensor must still find the press).

### 5. §IX's probability shaping is orphaned by the test and by the falsification clause

- **Where:** l.94 (the promise: "The ninth section will say what happens when the intermediary … begins to shape the answers"); l.196–198 and l.202 (the payoff); l.250–262 (seven questions, none about what the intermediary shapes or whether the record remains reachable beneath the synthesis); l.294 (falsification stated wholly in terms of identity, signatures, and searchable records); l.298 ("primary documents that a synthesis cannot silently revise" as a bare clause).
- **The problem:** The second reader's finding, which I adopt in full. A future that passes l.294, with local inference untouched and hosted records minimal, could still be the world of l.202: continuously re-ranked, silently corrected synthesis with the disagreeable record no longer reachable. The essay's most original worry is undetectable by its own instrument. The material for the fix is already in the record: OpenAI's Aug. 26 postmortem commits to chain-of-thought monitoring of "all tool-enabled inference workloads" for its most capable models (policy report, uncited work 13), which is the composed system's first real component and belongs at l.286.
- **Why it matters here:** The reader who finds §IX the best section (both readers did) reaches §XII and finds it forgotten.
- **Direction:** An eighth question, archival reachability and disclosure of change: does the primary record remain reachable beneath the synthesis, and is an alteration in output discoverable and contestable by the person who received the earlier one? Add a matching clause to l.294. This also gives l.298's "primary documents" clause something to hang on and gives §XV the substance it lacks. If the author declines, l.94 and l.196–202 must be cut back to what §XII can handle, which would be a loss.

### 6. The opening exhibit does state-shaped work with a corporate-shaped fact, and its chronology is compressed

- **Where:** l.16 ("why it may be dangerous for intelligence to be governable only through institutions"); l.276 ("the Hugging Face engineers were inconvenienced by a design choice, not by a regime"); l.10 ("In July 2026 … circumvented the controls"; "published on August 26"); l.12 ("The second revelation followed within the week"; "which models refused"); l.244 ("Only a rule aimed at the third and fourth layers needs to know what a person asked").
- **The problem:** Three things. (a) The prologue's moral at l.16 is a claim about *institutions* and by implication the state; §V then argues corporate refusal and state power are "different species," and l.276 retracts the framing outright without going back to repair it (second reader, objection i). What the second half actually demonstrates is Pettit's point, dependence on a guardian who is not answerable, plus the practical value of exit. (b) The chronology. OpenAI's own timeline (ledger C1) puts the first unintended internet access on May 26, the Hugging Face intrusion on July 9–13, the first disclosure on July 21, and the full account on Aug. 26; "within the week" (l.12), placed after the Aug. 26 date, reads as if the second revelation followed the first by days when it preceded the full account by a month. "Refused" should be "refused a large part" (the timeline's words, ledger C6/policy report §5); the quotation at l.12 silently splices out a clause (C5); and the deflationary reading ("they escaped because someone left the door open," Klonick, Lawfare) deserves one conceding clause at l.10, since the safeguards were reduced deliberately for the evaluation. (c) The exhibit undercuts l.244. The Hugging Face forensics *are* 17,600 logged agent actions; layer-5 enforcement is transcript-reading, and when the agent is a person's assistant the transcript is the cognitive record. The clean separation of "interfaces to the physical world" from "what a person asked" does not survive the essay's own opening.
- **Why it matters here:** The prologue is the piece's best-documented passage and the one the trade press will check first against the record it already knows.
- **Direction:** Rewrite l.16 as the non-domination exhibit and reserve the sovereign-power claim for §V, where it is earned. Fix the dates per ledger C1 (a sentence: found its way onto the internet in late May; went through Hugging Face in July; disclosed July 21; accounted for Aug. 26) and move "within the week" to the incident it belongs to. Add the SaferAI corroboration at l.14 so the exit half no longer rests on "Hugging Face's account alone." At l.244, concede that layer-5 enforcement reads transcripts, and say what follows: the incident-record rule of l.254 must be scoped to the agent's *actions*, not the person's *deliberation*, which is a distinction the essay can draw and no one else has.

### 7. The contribution is starved, and the piece cannot stand alone

- **Where:** section lengths (§I 1,276 words; §XIII 300; §XV 338; 62 percent of the body before §X); §IV (l.98–108) and §XV (l.298–300), which assemble the same thinkers twice; l.148 (the Church Committee paragraph that announces itself as perfunctory); l.204 ("That is the fallibilism of the first section again"); the coda's three endings (l.308, l.310, l.312); and the nine imports from the prior essays at l.46, l.66, l.90, l.148, l.152, l.204, l.248, l.280, l.306.
- **The problem:** The author's long form spends 30–50 percent of its length before the contemporary payoff; this spends 62, and the cost lands on the sections that are the piece. §XV is §IV with three names added and no new distinction; the Church Committee paragraph summarizes the previous essay and is used by nothing later; the Williams paragraph confesses its own repetition. The coda ends three times, and the middle ending (l.310, "No artificial model is identical with reason…") belongs to *The Constitution of Reason*. On the imports: the second reader, cold, found nine load-bearing moves outsourced to essays the reader has not seen, several of which "settle" rather than gesture ("does not need re-arguing here," l.90; "takes the point as settled," l.148; the invisible "seven criteria," l.248). The persona's continuity file records that the essays have never cited each other; the drafting note flags this departure as open. It is a deliberate departure, and possibly a welcome one, but as executed it produces an essay with holes where the reader is sent elsewhere.
- **Why it matters here:** A reader at an ideas publication reads cold. And the constructive sections are where the essay is most original (legal report §2; policy report §2): the four-condition complex and permissioned cognition have no counterpart found anywhere.
- **Direction:** Cut roughly 2,000 words from §I (to ~800: Bayle and Locke in one paragraph; keep Kant and Mill at length because the essay uses them later), §IV/§XV (either make §XV the place the thinkers are *used* on the layered system, or cut it to its first paragraph), the Church Committee paragraph, and the Williams paragraph (or move Accuracy and Sincerity to §XV as the positive vocabulary that section lacks). Spend the words on §XIII (run the test on one hard proposal the way l.266 runs it on the easy one), on Major issue 1's paragraph, and on Major issue 5's eighth question. Internalize the three imports that carry weight in two or three sentences each (chilling as the injury that leaves no defendant; behavioral legibility defined once without self-quotation; "no finite mind is reason as such") and cut the rest, or keep the cross-references and accept the cost knowingly. Cut l.310 to a clause inside l.308; l.312's last two sentences are the right last two sentences. ## The discursive situation

The essay enters two conversations and knows one of them well. In the AI-policy stratum it is better situated than its citations show: the policy actors are characterized accurately and in three cases (OpenAI, Anthropic, the Order) more generously than the surrounding discourse; the Stationers' genealogy is original on every search run; the five layers ordered by what each must know, the four-condition complex, and permissioned cognition have no found counterpart; and the "privacy-preserving verification" literature (Sastry, Heim, Shavit, flexHEG, CNAS) is not a counterargument but the essay's own position in the other side's jersey, which the essay must cite or be accused of reinventing (policy report §4(c)). In the freedom-of-thought and privacy-law stratum the essay is an outsider who re-derived the founding article (Blitz 2010), stepped into a scope debate it does not know is running (Bublitz 2013; Ligthart 2025), and dropped the doctrine (third-party) and the record (chat-log litigation) that its own legally literate readers will expect.
