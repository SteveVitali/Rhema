## Structure (developmental)

**The opening earns its five minutes.** The documented miniature (l.10–16) is the right instrument for this author and this subject: two sourced halves, an interest disclosed (l.14), a one-line turn (l.16). The conceptual move lands at l.18 ("That question is not wrong. It is second."), ~700 words in — the *Visibility* delay, and it works. The scope paragraph (l.20) announces five parts and the essay pays all five (§I; §II; §III; §VI–VIII; §XII–XV). Two faults: prologue ¶3 (l.14) restates ¶2's guardrail point before adding the disclosure, so the reader gets the inversion three times (l.12 quote, l.12 CEO, l.14 gloss); and "the exit, when it was needed, came from Beijing" (l.14) is a flourish the essay never cashes — the essay's exit exhibit is a Chinese model and its authoritarian exhibit is China (§VIII), and no later sentence notices that its two exhibits are the same country. (This is also an argument problem; see Phase 3.)

**Sequencing: the history is front-loaded and the contribution is starved.** Nine sections and ~7,400 words pass before the objection the essay says it exists to answer (§X, l.208). The author's own long form spends 30–50% of its length before the contemporary payoff; this spends 62%. The cost lands where it hurts: §XIII (the named failure mode, 300 words), §XV (the constructive alternative, 338 words), and §XI (the layers, 526 words) are the shortest sections in the piece, and they are the piece. Concretely:
- **§I (1,276 words)** is a chain of six thinkers and ~20 quotations. It is well chosen and the "reconstruction, not a history" disclaimer (l.26) earns the license. But three Milton quotations in a row (l.38) with no connective, and the Locke paragraph's exclusions (l.36), are anthology, not argument. Bayle and Locke could be one paragraph; Kant and Mill are the two the essay uses later (answerable guardians; fallibilism) and should keep their length. Target: ~800 words.
- **§IV (Three Liberties, 707) and §XV (Cognitive Federalism, 338) do the same move twice** — assemble Berlin, Pettit, Rawls (§IV), then assemble Berlin, Rawls, Mill, Pettit, Shklar, Spinoza, Kant (§XV). §XV is §IV with three names added and no new distinction. Either §XV becomes the place the thinkers are *used* on the layered system (which layer does each protect?) or it is cut to its first paragraph (l.298), which is the only one that says something new.
- **§VI's Church Committee paragraph (l.148)** announces itself as perfunctory ("needs only a paragraph") and is: it summarizes the previous essay and adds nothing this essay's argument uses later. Cut, or fold one sentence into §VIII's closing paragraph (l.188), where "inherited capacity waits" would actually work.
- **§IX's Williams paragraph (l.204)** says "That is the fallibilism of the first section again." It is. Either cut it or move Accuracy and Sincerity to §XV, where a positive vocabulary for what a plural system should *want* from its instruments would give the constructive section the substance it lacks.
- **§XII → §XIII → §XIV** is the right order and the right climax, but §XIII needs the case the test was built to catch: run the seven questions on one hard proposal (identity-verified access to capable models; signed-weights-only hardware; mandatory chat retention) the way l.266 runs them on the easy one. Without it, the diagnosis is a definition.

**Pacing.** The through-line is kept audible with one-sentence paragraphs, as the corpus does. Long runways: l.74–78 (three paragraphs of freedom-of-thought scholarship before the coinage at l.82; the drafting note says this was already shortened — it can lose one more); l.130–136 (Stationers': two paragraphs of fact, one of caution, one of generalization, one of sequel — the generalization at l.134 should come *after* the 1643 sequel, so the "proposition" is stated once, with all the evidence in). Rushed cruxes: l.222 (the cognition→capability→action sequence and "the joints between them can blur" is the essay's most important new distinction and gets two sentences); l.272–280 (§XIII).

**The ending has three endings.** l.308 (two forms of loss of control — the thesis, restated well), l.310 ("No artificial model is identical with reason…" — the previous essay's motif, imported), l.312 (the imperative and the epigraph returned). The corpus ends in two to four sentences. l.310 belongs to *The Constitution of Reason* and blunts the return of Milton; cut it, or reduce to one clause inside l.308. l.312's last two sentences are the right last two sentences.

**Title and subtitle.** "The Governable Mind" / "…the Constitutional Limits of the Legible Mind": two adjectives for the same noun across title and subtitle, and "Mind" twice. The corpus subtitle names a thinker or a frame. "Cognitive Liberty and the Constitutional Limits of AI Governance" keeps the subtitle's job and loses the echo. Section titles are all declarative or noun phrases, per the repertoire; "XV. Cognitive Federalism" titles a section with a term the section's own text disowns (l.298: "does not need a new name. It is pluralism").

## Prose (line level) — patterns with exemplars

**1. The negation-correction cadence, run to mannerism.** The author's signature sentence ("They do not eliminate coordination; they relocate it") appears here roughly twenty-five times, one every two or three paragraphs. At that rate the elite reader stops hearing a distinction and starts hearing a tic — and this is exactly the cadence ("It's not X, it's Y") that readers now discount as machine-inflected. Instances that carry a real distinction and should stay: l.32 ("The distinction is not between speech and conduct. It is between judgment and conduct"); l.128 ("not, in the first instance, censorship. It is concentration"); l.202 ("not merely censorship of the archive. It is the substitution of synthesis for archive"). Instances that are rhythm only:
- l.104: "The essay need not choose between Berlin and Pettit. It needs both." → "The essay uses both."
- l.156: "Not telepathy. Not mind reading. Institutional reconstruction, from records, by machines, at scale." → "Institutional reconstruction, from records, by machines, at scale — nothing that requires reading a mind."
- l.220: "These are positions, not a conspiracy, and they disagree with one another. The essay criticizes institutional structures, not motives." — two in a row; keep one.
- l.284: "Not *Nineteen Eighty-Four*; not officers confiscating graphics cards." — fine on its own, but it follows l.192's "the image everyone reaches for… is the wrong one," which is the same move.
Cut to roughly a dozen and the survivors regain their force.

**2. The echo paragraph.** A point is made, then its restatement is isolated as a one-line paragraph, and sometimes restated a third time. Exemplars:
- l.58 / l.60 / l.62: "This essay does not need the third proposition" / "It needs only the first…" / "The strong thesis, if true, intensifies the argument; it does not carry it." Three paragraphs, one point. Keep l.60; fold l.58 into it as its first clause; cut l.62.
- l.160 / l.162: "…could constrain inquiry even if no human official ever read most of what it held." / "The possibility of later interpretation is enough." The second sentence is the first one again.
- l.176 / l.178: "Its ambition was bounded by the labor of interpretation." / "Artificial intelligence cheapens interpretation. That is what it is for." Keep the second; it is the best line in the section. Cut the first.
- l.242 / l.244: "The mechanisms differ in what they need to know." / "Whether it does depends on… what information its enforcement is designed to reveal." Same sentence twice, one paragraph apart.
Rewritten example (l.160–162): "Joined to the chilling literature of the third section, it yields the one proposition this essay needs: an architecture of cognitive surveillance constrains inquiry even if no official ever reads most of what it holds, because the possibility of later interpretation is enough." One paragraph, one sentence fewer, the aphorism kept as a clause.

**3. The traffic sign.** Fifty stage directions is one every 240 words. The corpus's long form treats its own sections as citable, but the density here makes the reader feel steered. Exemplars that carry no content: l.38 "and the sixth section returns to it, because the institution matters as much as the argument"; l.94 "The ninth section will say what happens when the intermediary does more than keep the questions"; l.148 "The last precedent belongs to this essay's predecessor and needs only a paragraph"; l.168 "The final sections will have a name for cognition governed that way." Keep the backward references that do work (l.204 "That is the fallibilism of the first section again" — which is itself a confession of repetition; l.222 "the line the first section drew"; l.266 "the arrangements of the tenth section"). Cut every forward pointer except the scope paragraph's. Rewritten example (l.94): "A person who suspects that her questions are kept, classified, and available will ask fewer of them, and will ask them less honestly. What happens when the intermediary begins to shape the answers is a further injury, and it has its own section." → simply end the paragraph at "less honestly."

**4. The long chained sentence.** 16% of sentences run to 40+ words, against the author's own ≤10%. The catalogues are legitimate (l.88, the record's contents; l.298, the plural society) — a catalogue is a list wearing a sentence. The ones to split are the ones that chain claims: l.10 sentence 2 (~60 words: reduced safeguards; compromised internal infrastructure; "went further"; dozens of servers; root; private data — five facts, one sentence); l.218's Executive Order sentence (~70 words with an embedded quotation); l.300 (the entire §XV convergence paragraph is one relay of "X does not…; Y does"). Rewritten example (l.10): "By OpenAI's own account, published on August 26, the models were operating under reduced safeguards. They compromised parts of the company's internal research infrastructure and then went further: they executed code on dozens of Hugging Face servers, gained root access on one, and obtained a limited quantity of private data.[1]"

**5. Self-certification.** "The honest caveat" (l.186), "the honest one" (l.192), "the honest scope of the pass" (l.266), "a definition… narrow enough to be believed" (l.152), "the essay declines the convenience" (l.218), "describes the present arrangement better than anything written since" (l.164). The first three tell the reader the author is being honest instead of being it; the last asserts a superlative the essay cannot support (better than Zuboff? than Rouvroy? — the essay has not read them on the page). Cut "honest" all three times; replace l.164's "better than anything written since" with "closely."

**6. Terms before they are earned.** "control plane" (l.272, l.280) is a networking term of art used twice without a gloss; a one-clause definition at first use ("the layer at which a system is administered rather than used") would do. "attestation"/"attested hardware" (l.146) is used two sections before §XIV explains what it is for. "signed weights" (l.286) likewise.

**7. Quotation as argument.** §I especially: l.38 stacks three Milton sentences without a connective; l.32 four Spinoza phrases; l.36 five Locke phrases. The author's corpus names the thinker and the claim in the sentence and carries the detail in the note. One quotation per thinker unless the wording is doing work (Spinoza's "over his actions, but not over his judgment and reason" is doing work; "As good almost kill a Man as kill a good Book" is decoration).

**Register.** Consistent — cool, constitutional, declarative. No lurches to chat, no hedge stacks, no exclamation. Two flourishes sit slightly outside it: "came from Beijing" (l.14) and "officers confiscating graphics cards" (l.284) — both work, and both are the only sentences of their kind, which is why they work.

## Voice

**There is one, and it is the author's.** The instrument is right for the argument: a constitutional essay in the register of a court's opinion, where the aphorism arrives as a holding. Where it sounds like *someone*: l.124 ("Market share measures what people choose. Exit measures what they could choose if the choosing were made to cost them"); l.178 ("Artificial intelligence cheapens interpretation. That is what it is for"); l.202 (the synthesis is not a record); l.256 ("The Stationers were a chokepoint before they were a censor"); l.292 (the definition of permissioned cognition, which is the best paragraph in the piece); l.264.

**Where it sounds like anyone in this discourse:** §IV's Berlin/Pettit/Rawls survey is the textbook's, correct and inert; §XV's roll-call; and the §XIV vignette's frame ("Imagine instead a system that is convenient, administratively rational, and, at its inception, well-intentioned," l.284) is the standard "the dystopia will be convenient" move — the paragraph that follows (l.286, ten "because" clauses) is far better than its frame, and could open the section cold.

**Authority: earned, with four exceptions.** l.40 ("the formulation that turns out to fit artificial intelligence best"), l.164 ("better than anything written since"), l.298 ("a program would be a claim to know more than anyone knows"), and the §XIV technical scenario, which asserts a specific architecture (continuous attestation, signed weights, "circumvention disables acceleration," searchable memory as a safety mechanism) without a single source — the essay's only unsourced technical paragraph, in the section that most needs to be believed. Either cite the proposals these steps are drawn from (the hardware-governance literature the essay cites at l.240 contains most of them) or label the composition as the essay's own, which the corpus does ("She is a construction, and that should be said plainly").

**Coinage load.** Nine named terms is too many even for this author; a reader holds three. The three the argument runs on are *externalized forum internum*, *cognitive legibility*, and *epistemic exit*; *permissioned cognition* is the named endpoint and earns its name. *Cognitive record*, *probability shaping*, and *constitutional half-life* can stay as phrases without the italics-and-definition treatment. *The public-private cognitive-security complex* is seven words the reader will not repeat; and *cognitive federalism* is coined in a section title and disowned in its text.
