# rauch-constitution-of-knowledge
- **URL:** https://www.persuasion.community/p/jonathan-rauch-the-constitution-of · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v2→v3; the title-adjacent predecessor (Major issue 2)
- **What it establishes:** Jonathan Rauch, *The Constitution of Knowledge: A Defense of Truth* (Brookings Institution Press, 2021). Two constitutive rules: **fallibilist rule** — "No one gets the final say. You may claim that a statement is established as knowledge only if it can be debunked, in principle, and only insofar as it withstands attempts to debunk it"; **empirical rule** — "No one has personal authority… only insofar as the method used to check it gives the same result regardless of the identity of the checker." Explicit Madison analogy: the epistemic constitution, like the political one, "create[s] a governing structure, forcing social contestation onto peaceful and productive pathways." The book *defends an existing human institution* (the reality-based community) against attackers (disinformation, cancel culture, cultish isolation).
- **Reliability:** secondary (Persuasion excerpt/interview; consistent with Brookings book page per discourse report). Book itself not read — cite for its thesis at this granularity only.
- **Relevance:** Differentiation for v3: Rauch defends an existing, human, informal constitution under attack; this essay generalizes the *design problem* and extends it to artificial cognition, and (via the Logos) refuses Rauch's proceduralism — the checking procedures themselves remain judgeable.
