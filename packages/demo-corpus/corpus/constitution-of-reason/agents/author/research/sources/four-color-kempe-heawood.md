# four-color-kempe-heawood
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_color_theorem · **Accessed:** 2026-07-30 · **For:** v2→v3; historical concrete case (Major issue 3)
- **What it establishes:** Kempe published a proof of the four-color theorem in 1879 (*American Journal of Mathematics*), widely acclaimed and accepted for 11 years. Heawood (1890) exposed the flaw (the Kempe-chain argument fails on degree-5 vertices) and salvaged the five-color theorem from the wreck. Appel & Haken announced a computer-assisted proof June 21, 1976 — first major theorem proved with extensive computer assistance, "infeasible for a human to check by hand," hence the checkability controversy. Werner & Gonthier formalized the proof in the Coq proof assistant in 2005, removing dependence on trusting individual verification programs.
- **Reliability:** secondary (Wikipedia), but for undisputed textbook history; adequate.
- **Relevance:** The human-institution thread of the concrete case: a legible proof error that an entire community failed to see for a decade until an unpersuaded reader looked; the salvage (five-color) as productive partial result; Appel–Haken and Coq as the verification/legibility chapter.
