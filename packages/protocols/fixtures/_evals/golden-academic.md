# Retrieval practice improves retention of procedural skills: a registered report proposal

## Background

Retrieval practice — the deliberate act of recalling information from memory —
produces larger long-term retention gains than equivalent time spent restudying.
The effect is well established for declarative material: in Roediger and
Karpicke's 2006 comparison, students who practiced recall outperformed students
who restudied after a one-week delay, despite reporting lower confidence during
learning. Whether the effect generalizes to procedural skills is less clear.
Meta-analytic estimates for motor and procedural domains are smaller and more
heterogeneous, and several registered reports have failed to detect a benefit
at all.

## Proposed study

We propose a 2×2 randomized design (N = 240 undergraduates) crossing practice
type (retrieval vs. restudy) with retention interval (1 day vs. 7 days) on a
standardized knot-tying task scored by trained raters blind to condition. The
primary outcome is error-free completion time at the delayed test; the
secondary outcome is sequence-accuracy under a verbal-shadowing load,
registered as a test of transfer under divided attention.

## Expected contribution

A positive result would extend retrieval-practice theory beyond verbal
material and inform the spacing policies used in clinical skills curricula. A
null result would sharpen the boundary conditions of an effect often taught as
domain-general. Because the design, outcome, and analysis plan are registered
in advance, either outcome is interpretable; the study's value rests on the
precommitment, not on the direction of the result.

## Methods note

All exclusions and the stopping rule are fixed in the registered protocol.
Raters will be blind to condition and retention interval; inter-rater
reliability will be reported with its confidence interval.
